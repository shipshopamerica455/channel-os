## 4. What to publish instead

**Long ambient compilations, 1–8 hours, search-titled.** Two to three per week.
This is the whole strategy. Everything else is decoration.

The rule for each upload: it must be something a person deliberately *leaves running* —
while sleeping, studying, working, or winding down. Length is not padding here; length
is the product.

Title for search intent, not for cleverness. The queries that carry this category are
literal: *"3 hours relaxing piano for sleep"*, *"baroque music for studying"*,
*"classical music for deep sleep black screen"*. Your current titles
(*"Find Your Calm: Bach's Healing Harmony 🌿"*) are brand-voice titles for a channel
nobody is searching for yet.

**The micro-niche is decided: functional focus music for traders and high-stakes work**,
with sleep and relaxation kept as the back catalogue. Chosen 2026-07-25 from the
blue-ocean run — see [NICHE.md](NICHE.md) for the evidence and [CALENDAR-30-DAY.md](CALENDAR-30-DAY.md)
for the schedule.

Two reasons it beat the alternatives. The data: three recent breakouts from channels
under 20k subs (523k views at 9.4k subs, 725k at 19k subs, both inside the last year) —
the strongest buy signal the method recognises. And the economics: **traders are the
highest-CPM audience on YouTube**, so once the gate is cleared, the same watch hour is
worth several times what a generic sleep hour pays. Since the gate is the near-term
problem and RPM is the entire long-term game, this niche is the only candidate that
answers both.

The bridge from the existing catalogue is real: Mellow Melodies is already baroque, and
*"60 BPM Baroque for linear thinking"* is that same music with a functional promise
attached rather than a mood.

## 5. The honest risk, stated plainly

AI-generated ambient music over static AI images is **precisely** the profile YouTube's
July 2025 inauthentic-content policy update was written to catch. Channels in this
category do get rejected at review. Anyone promising you otherwise is selling something.

What separates the ones that pass:

1. **You own the music.** Generated audio is yours — no Content ID claim, no revenue
   diverted to a rights holder. Your existing pipeline already qualifies: the hymn SOP
   renders public-domain MIDI locally, and the "Moonlit" tracks are generated. Adding
   FlowMusic keeps this property. **Do not** put other people's recordings of Bach on
   this channel — the compositions are public domain, the recordings are not.
2. **The visuals move and vary.** A single still image looped for three hours reads as
   automated. Generated video (Veo, via the same useapi token) does not.
3. **Nothing published looks unattended.** See §3 — that is why those fixes come first.
4. **Sequencing is a real editorial act.** Track order, key relationships, and pacing
   across a 3-hour bed is the human contribution, and it is worth making visible in the
   description.

This is a real risk, not a blocker. Proceed with it in view.
