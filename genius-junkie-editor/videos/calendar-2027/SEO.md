## Pinned comment

> The one-second check, so you never ship a wrong grid:
>
> **February 2027 starts on a Monday and has 28 days — and 28 days is exactly
> four weeks.** So on a Monday-start grid it fills a perfect **4 × 7 block**:
> four rows, seven columns, completely full, no blank squares at the beginning
> and none at the end. It is the only month of the year that does that, and it
> only works this year.
>
> Build February first and look at it. One gap anywhere in that block means your
> grid is wrong — and if February is wrong, the other eleven are too.
>
> And the reason last year's file cannot be reused: 365 days is 52 weeks and one
> day left over, so every date slides one square right. 1 Jan 2026 is a
> Thursday; 1 Jan 2027 is a Friday. Reuse the layout, the fonts and the
> pictures. Never reuse the numbers.
>

The arithmetic that catches people building next year's calendar:

A common year is 365 days = 52 weeks + 1 day, so every date lands exactly ONE weekday later the following year. 1 Jan 2026 was a Thursday, so 1 Jan 2027 is a **Friday**.

2027 is not a leap year. 2028 is — so next year's shift will be two days, not one.

The self-check: February 2027 starts on a Monday. If yours doesn't, you have built the wrong year.
