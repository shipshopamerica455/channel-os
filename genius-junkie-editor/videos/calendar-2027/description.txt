A 2027 calendar is twelve grids and a picture. The pictures are the easy half.
The grids are the half that has to be correct — and if you build them by reusing
last year's file, every single date on all twelve pages is wrong.

📅 **Why it shifts:** a common year is 365 days, which is 52 weeks and one day
left over. That one spare day pushes every date one square to the right the
following year. January 1st 2026 is a **Thursday**; January 1st 2027 is a
**Friday**. Christmas Day moves from a Friday to a Saturday.

🚩 So if you open last year's design and edit the year in the title, you have not
made a 2027 calendar. You have made a **2026 calendar with the wrong year printed
on it** — and it looks completely normal. Nothing turns red. Somebody prints it,
sticks it on a wall, and books an appointment on the wrong day. **Reuse the
layout, the fonts and the pictures. Never reuse the numbers.**

✅ **The one-second check:** February 2027 starts on a Monday and has 28 days,
and 28 days is exactly four weeks — so on a Monday-start grid it fills a perfect
**4 × 7 block**. Four rows, seven columns, completely full, no blanks at either
end. It is the only month of the year that does that, and it only works this
year. Build February, look at it: one gap anywhere and your grid is wrong, which
means the other eleven are too.

⚠️ One thing to know before you start: Canva's automatic **"add dates" feature
only exists in Canva Sheets and Docs** — not in a print design. In a wall
calendar you are building the grid yourself with a table, which is exactly what
this video walks through.

🖨️ **Printing:** export as **PDF Print** (300 dpi), not PDF Standard (96 dpi).
And make two files — bleed **ON** for a print shop, bleed **OFF** for the home
printable, because a home printer cannot print to the edge and bleed gives your
buyer a white border and a cropped picture.
