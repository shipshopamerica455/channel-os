## Pinned comment

> The whole video in one paragraph, if you skip the rest:
>
> **Your phone crops the width, not the height.** It scales a wallpaper until
> the height fills the screen and throws away whatever hangs off the sides. So
> a 16:9 file — the **1080 × 1920** that almost every wallpaper template ships
> at — loses about **18% of its width** on an iPhone 16 Pro or a Galaxy S25,
> and **over 20%** on a Pixel 9. What survives is upscaled, so it is softer too.
>
> Design at **1440 × 3120** for phone and **3840 × 2160** for desktop, and every
> device scales *down* instead. Downscaling is the safe direction.
>
> Two more things nobody mentions:
> · the only reliably visible band is the **middle** — the clock owns the top
>   third, the dock owns the bottom
> · **DPI does not exist on a screen**. Ignore the box. You set the pixels when
>   you made the canvas.
>
> And if you are planning to sell a pack: Canva's licence says you *"can't sell
> any Canva content on a standalone basis"* — a stock photo exported at
> wallpaper size is exactly that. Your own composition is a product; their asset
> lightly edited is not.
>
> Full kit (both templates + the size cheat sheet + the licence page):
> https://geniusjunkie.net/wallpaper-kit/

## Facts, and where they came from

- **Canva's own wallpaper page** still says *"Standard wallpaper has a resolution
  of 1024 x 768 and a screen ratio of 4:3."* — quoted on screen, and the reason
  the video tells people to ignore Canva's own guidance here.
- Canva publishes **no pixel dimension** for its phone wallpaper templates; it
  claims they look "sharp on any device". 1080 × 1920 is the community default,
  not a Canva-stated size — the script is careful about that distinction.
- **Crop percentages are computed, not typed** — `crop_pct()` in
  `product/make_wallpaper_pack.py` derives them from the device table, so the
  cheat sheet and the video cannot drift apart. Measured: iPhone 16 Pro 18.2%,
  iPhone 16 18.0%, Galaxy S25 17.9%, Pixel 9 20.8%.
- The licence quotes are from Canva's commercial-use help page, linked in the
  description so a buyer can check them.
