## S02 — THE 3-SECOND RULE (~125 words)

> Before any design, three things cover designers know. One: your cover is
> bought at thumbnail size — about eighty pixels tall in search results. If
> the title isn't readable when you squint, the design has failed, whatever
> it looks like full-screen. Two: genre is a promise. Thrillers look like
> thrillers, romance looks like romance — a cover that's "unique" in the
> wrong way reads as amateur, and browsers scroll past. Three: the spine and
> back aren't decoration — on Amazon nobody sees them, but every "Look
> Inside" and every real-world bookshelf does. Design all three panels like
> they'll be seen, because they will. Keep those in mind and the same book
> sells more.

## S03 — THE SPINE MATHS AMAZON USES (~150 words)

> Now the part most tutorials skip — the maths. Your cover is one single
> image: back cover, plus spine, plus front cover, plus bleed. The front
> and back are your trim size — we're using six by nine. The spine depends
> on your page count and paper: white paper is zero point zero zero two two
> five two inches per page; cream paper is slightly thicker at zero point
> zero zero two five. Our notebook is one hundred and twenty white pages —
> that's a spine of zero point two seven inches. Add bleed — an eighth of
> an inch on every outside edge. So the full canvas is twelve point five
> two inches wide by nine point two five tall. Two decimals wrong and the
> previewer rejects it, so let the numbers do the work — they're in the
> description and baked into the template pack.

[VISUAL: motion-graphics equation builds: 0.125 + 6 + (120 × 0.002252) + 6
+ 0.125 = 12.52 × 9.25; white vs cream chips; spine grows as pages counter
climbs.]

## S04 — CANVAS SETUP + THE THREE ZONES (~130 words)

> Into Canva: Create a design, Custom size, switch the units to inches, and
> type twelve point five two by nine point two five. One page, three zones.
> Drag two vertical guides — the spine sits dead centre, six point one two
> five inches in from each side. File, Settings, Show print bleed, so you
> can see the cut line. And a safety rule the previewer enforces: keep text
> at least a quarter inch inside every trim edge, and remember the left
> panel is the BACK cover, the right panel is the FRONT — new designers
> flip them constantly because the wrap reads back-to-front. Label the
> zones with a text box each while you work; delete the labels before
> export.

[VISUAL: custom size dialog types 12.52 × 9.25 in; guides snap to spine;
BACK · SPINE · FRONT labels; flipped-panels ✕ card.]

## S05 — BUILD THE FRONT (~170 words)

> The front cover carries the sale, so build it first — top of the type
> stack: the title, big, in the top third. One hundred and twenty points is
> a starting size, not a maximum — if it fills the width, you're close.
> Under it the subtitle, a third of the size, and the author name anchored
> at the bottom. That's the classic hierarchy: title, subtitle, author —
> three sizes, three weights, one font family or two at most. For our
> dotted notebook we'll keep it typographic: a deep ink background, cream
> type, one accent rule under the title. If you use imagery, it sits
> BEHIND the type, and the type still wins the squint test. Nudge
> everything a quarter inch inside the trim, then do the thumbnail check:
> zoom out until the front is the size of your thumb. Title still
> readable? Then it works on Amazon.

## S06 — THE BACK COVER (~130 words)

> The back cover, left panel. Structure, top to bottom: a one-line hook in
> larger type — a question or a promise. Then the blurb: three short
> paragraphs maximum, what it is, who it's for, why this one. Then, if you
> have it, a one-line author bio. And the corner you must leave alone:
> Amazon prints its barcode in the bottom-right of the back cover — a two
> by one point two inch block. KDP drops it on top of whatever's there, so
> keep that corner clear of text and busy artwork; a flat colour is fine.
> If you use your own ISBN barcode, same corner, same size. Design around
> it and nothing gets covered.

[VISUAL: back panel builds hook → blurb → bio; barcode zone stamps
bottom-right with a keep-clear hatch; barcode-over-text ✕ example.]

## S07 — THE SPINE (~120 words)

> The spine, centre strip. First rule: Amazon only allows spine text if
> the book has at least seventy-nine pages — under that, leave it blank.
> Ours has one hundred and twenty, so we get text. Add the title, rotate
> it ninety degrees so it reads top to bottom, and size it to leave at
> least a sixteenth of an inch of clearance on each side of the strip —
> cramped spine text is the number one previewer complaint. Author
> surname at the base if it fits. Match the front's font and colour so
> the wrap reads as one object, not three stickers. On the shelf, the
> spine IS your cover — make it confident.

[VISUAL: spine strip zooms; text rotates into place; clearance callipers
animate 0.0625" each side; shelf cutaway.]

## S08 — THE SQUINT LAB (~130 words)

> Before export, three fast checks that catch almost every amateur cover.
> Shrink it: front cover at thumbnail size — title readable, author
> readable, nothing else needs to be. Mono it: drop a black-and-white
> filter for a second — if the type disappears, your contrast is too low,
> whatever the colours look like. And shelf it: put your cover next to
> five bestsellers in your niche — same genre signals, better execution,
> is the target. Different is only good when it's deliberate. Canva makes
> all three checks free: duplicate the page, shrink, desaturate, compare.
> Ten minutes here saves a cover that quietly kills the listing for
> months.

## S09 — EXPORT + THE PREVIEWER (~135 words)

> Export time. Share, Download, PDF Print — covers must be PDF, and tick
> Flatten PDF so every layer bakes into one. Crop marks OFF for KDP — the
> bleed is already in your canvas size, and marks confuse the previewer.
> Upload it in your KDP dashboard under Book Content, cover section, and
> then actually run the Launch Previewer — it overlays the trim, bleed,
> and barcode zones on your file and tells you exactly what's wrong while
> it's still free to fix. Green ticks, approve, done. If it flags text
> outside the safe zone, you know precisely which quarter-inch rule it
> means — nudge, re-export, re-upload. Two minutes, not two days of
> support emails.

[VISUAL: download dialog — PDF Print + Flatten ticked, crop marks OFF

## S10 — THE EBOOK COVER (~110 words)

> Selling the Kindle version too? Different file: the ebook cover is
> front-only, no spine, no back — ideally two thousand five hundred and
> sixty by one thousand six hundred pixels, a one point six to one ratio.
> Don't rebuild it: duplicate your design, crop to the front panel, Resize
> to the ebook dimensions, and nudge the type to the new proportions.
> Same title stack, same colours — buyers should recognise it's the same
> book at a glance. Export that one as JPEG, high quality. One design,
> two products, five extra minutes.

## S11 — THE CHECKLIST (~85 words)

> Before you hit publish, six checks. One: canvas maths matches your page
> count and paper — spine included. Two: title passes the thumbnail
> squint test. Three: back corner clear for the barcode. Four: spine text
> only if seventy-nine plus pages, with clearance. Five: PDF Print,
> flattened, crop marks off. Six: previewer run, all green. Six ticks and
> your cover is ready for the world's biggest bookshop.
