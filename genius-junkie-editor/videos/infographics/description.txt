Canva will not let you make a tall infographic. Their maximum design size is
8000 × 3125 pixels, and that is per axis — so the second number is your height
ceiling.

Three thousand one hundred and twenty five pixels, for a format whose whole
point is that it scrolls. Type a taller number into the custom size box and the
fields turn red and refuse you — no warning text explaining why, no suggestion,
no offer to scale it. Most people meet that wall halfway through, delete a
section, and ship something thinner than they meant.

The way round it is arithmetic: design small, export big. A Canva design
downloads as a PNG at between 0.5× and 3.125× its size, so build at 450 × 3000,
set the multiplier to 2×, and out comes the 900 × 6000 file you were told you
could not make. The ceiling is on the canvas, never on the export.

THE ARITHMETIC
design size × multiplier = output
· 450 × 3000  ×  2×      =  900 × 6000
· 600 × 2400  ×  2.5×    =  1500 × 6000
· 640 × 3125  ×  3.125×  =  2000 × 9765
The consequence nobody checks: everything is a fraction of the pixels it will
end up being, so every photo you place needs AT LEAST the multiplier × the
resolution it looks like it needs. A photo exactly sharp at 450 wide will be
soft at 900.

CANVA'S PUBLISHED LIMITS
· Minimum design size 40 × 40 px; maximum 8000 × 3125 px — per axis.
· "If the values in the custom size fields turn red, it means the values are
  less than or exceed the size limits."
· A design can be downloaded in PNG format "from 0.5 up to 3.125 times its
  size".
(Canva Help Centre, "Resize designs and size limits".)

CANVA'S OWN THREE RECOMMENDED SHAPES
· Blog — 663 × 2000 px (a tall column)

One tall column and two landscapes. There is no single artwork that is both, so
the tall one is your master and the landscape ones are extracts — one section,
one number, re-laid out. Resize changes the canvas; it does not re-think the
layout. (canva.com/create/infographics.) Verified 6 October 2026.
