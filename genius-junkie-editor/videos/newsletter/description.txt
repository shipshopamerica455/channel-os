Every link in your Canva newsletter is probably dead, and nothing told you.

Canva's own help page says it plainly: "Hyperlinks will only work if you
download your design as a PDF (except flattened PDFs)", and "Image formats like
JPG and PNG do not support hyperlinks regardless of the image source". So if you
exported a JPG and pasted it into an email — which is what most people do —
every link in it is a picture of a link.

And here is the part that catches careful people. If your fonts came out wrong
on somebody else's machine and you ticked FLATTEN to fix them, which is Canva's
own suggestion, you just killed every link in the file a second time. Flattening
merges every layer into one image, and your links were layers.

This builds a newsletter properly: the page, the grid, the masthead, the blocks,
the export decision, and the five-minute test that catches all three things that
actually go wrong.

DO YOUR LINKS SURVIVE?
· In the Canva editor — YES (which is why nobody catches this)
· PDF Standard, 96 dpi — YES
· PDF Print, 300 dpi — YES
· PDF with FLATTEN ticked — NO
· JPG — NO
· PNG — NO
· Posted to social media — NO
Links can be put on text, elements, images and videos, and one can point at
another page inside the same design — but none of that survives an image export.

CANVA'S PUBLISHED WORDING
· "Hyperlinks will only work if you download your design as a PDF (except
  flattened PDFs)."
· "Image formats like JPG and PNG do not support hyperlinks regardless of the
  image source."
· Flattening "converts your design with multiple layers into one" and "ensures
  fonts display accurately and secures your design".
· PDF Standard: "for illustrations, text, and graphics with a resolution of
  96 dpi". PDF Print: "for printing with a resolution of 300 dpi, with bleed,
  crop marks, and color profile (RGB or CMYK) selection option".
(Canva Help Centre — Add hyperlinks; Download designs as flattened PDF; Choose
the right download file type.) Verified 10 October 2026.
