Do your links survive?

From the Canva Help Centre, "Add hyperlinks": "Hyperlinks will only work if you download your design as a PDF (except flattened PDFs)", and "Image formats like JPG and PNG do not support hyperlinks regardless of the image source".

  • In the Canva editor — yes (which is why nobody catches this)
  • PDF Standard, 96 dpi — yes
  • PDF Print, 300 dpi — yes
  • PDF with flatten ticked — no
  • JPG — no
  • PNG — no
  • Posted to social media — no

Links can be put on text, elements, images and videos, and one can point at another page inside the same design — but none of that survives an image export. If you built a newsletter, exported a JPG and pasted it into an email, every link in it is a picture of a link.

The flatten trap — why careful people get caught

Flatten is not an obscure setting. It is what Canva offers when your fonts render wrong on somebody else's machine, which is the most common newsletter complaint there is.

Flattening a PDF in Canva ensures fonts display accurately and secures your design.

It does that by converting "your design with multiple layers into one". Your links were layers.

So the fix for the fonts is the thing that kills the links — and nothing warns you either time. Not the export dialog, not the file.

Instead: leave flatten off, export twice, and open it on the machine the fonts were wrong on.

Ninety-six or three hundred — they are not the same file

From "Choose the right download file type":

  • PDF Standard — "for illustrations, text, and graphics with a resolution of 96 dpi". Smaller file; right for the email.
  • PDF Print — "for printing with a resolution of 300 dpi, with bleed, crop marks, and color profile (RGB or CMYK) selection option".

96 dpi is a screen resolution. A Standard PDF run off on a school or office printer comes out soft and slightly muddy, and there is no fixing it afterwards. If anybody is printing it, you need both — same design, two downloads, thirty seconds.

The phone test, and the send test

A PDF on a phone opens as the whole page, so an A4 page arrives at about the size of a playing card before anybody pinches to zoom.

  • Should survive: the masthead, the lead headline, and the shape of the blocks.
  • Body text turning into grey stripes is fine — that is what body text does.
  • Headlines gone grey too? They are too small, and nobody zooms in to find out what they say.

Then send it to yourself, open it on your phone, and click two links — one near the top, one near the bottom. Send from the account you will really use, to an address on a different provider from your own.

Seven checks before you send it

  • Every link clicked in the exported file, not in the editor
  • Flatten is off
  • You know whether this copy is the 96 or the 300, and who is getting which
  • The masthead is identical to last issue
  • Every block has the date, the time and the place in it — not only in the link
  • It still reads at phone size, and the headlines are not grey stripes
  • It came out of the template, not out of last month's issue

The first is the one that actually goes wrong — and it goes wrong silently, in the one file your readers get.
