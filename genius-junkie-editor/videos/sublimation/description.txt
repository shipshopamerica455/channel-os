There is no white ink in sublimation. None. Which means every white area in
your Canva design is not printed at all — it is simply whatever your blank
happens to be.

Design the normal Canva way, on a white background with white text, and you
have made something that works perfectly on a white mug and falls apart on
every other blank you own. Nothing fails. The printer does not misfire. The
white mug was hiding it the whole time.

And the thing nearly every tutorial gets backwards: "export transparent or
you'll get a white box" is a PRINT ON DEMAND rule, where a provider lays white
ink onto a dark shirt. Your sublimation printer has no white ink at all, so a
white background and a transparent background do exactly the same thing. Export
transparent anyway — not for the printer, for you.

THE RULES, FROM THE PEOPLE WHO MAKE THE KIT
· "Dye-sublimation printing requires polyester fabrics or polymer-coated
  surfaces." — Epson
· "Dye sublimation requires light-colored materials because there's no white
  ink in the standard process. White areas are inherent to the product
  itself." — Printify
· Sawgrass, who make the ink, say the same of their VersiFlex system: it does
  not print white, so "you will need to be smart about the colors of material
  you choose."
· "When your design is sent for printing, the colors are automatically
  converted from RGB values to the closest possible CMYK match." — Canva Help
  Centre
· Transparent background on download is "only available to Canva Pro, Canva
  Teams, Canva Business, Canva Education, and Canva for Nonprofits plans." —
  Canva Help Centre

THE SIZING
Inches × 300 = pixels. A 9 × 3.5 in mug wrap is 2700 × 1050. A 10 × 12 in
shirt area is 3000 × 3600. Your blank supplier publishes a template and THAT
is the number that counts — suppliers differ, and a mug wrap from one is not a
mug wrap from another.
