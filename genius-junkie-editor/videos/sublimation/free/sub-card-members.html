The one that ruins designs

There is no white ink. White is a window onto the blank.

Dye sublimation requires light-colored materials because there's no white ink in the standard process. White areas are inherent to the product itself. — Printify

Sawgrass, who make the ink, say the same of their VersiFlex system: it does not print with white, so you have to be careful about the colour of material you choose.

In Canva, white is a colour you pick. At the press it is the absence of ink. So:

  • White text on a dark shape works — the shape is ink, the letters are the blank showing through it.
  • White text on nothing is invisible. Blank on blank.
  • A white drop shadow, outline or glow does nothing at all.
  • Off-white, cream and the palest grey are ink — they do print, and on a cream blank that is a faint dirty patch.

The ten-second test: drop a mid-tone rectangle behind your whole design in Canva and look at what survives. Anything that changes colour was never ink.

The blank

Dye-sublimation printing requires polyester fabrics or polymer-coated surfaces. — Epson

Not a preference. The dye turns to gas and bonds with polyester. On cotton there is nothing for it to bond to, so it sits on the surface looking completely acceptable for about one wash — and then it is gone.

  • ✅ Polyester fabric, or a polymer-coated hard good (mug, tumbler, slate, metal sign)
  • ✅ Light blanks only — on a dark blank the ink is translucent and disappears into it
  • ❌ Cotton, or an uncoated mug from a supermarket — takes nothing at all

The size

inches × 300 = pixels
  • 9 × 3.5 in mug wrap → 2700 × 1050 px
  • 10 × 12 in shirt area → 3000 × 3600 px

Your blank supplier publishes a template, and that is the number that counts — suppliers differ, and a mug wrap from one is not a mug wrap from another.

In Canva: Custom size → switch the units to pixels → type it in. Never design small and scale up.

The export — and the myth

"Export transparent or you will get a white box" is a print on demand rule, where a provider lays white ink onto a dark shirt. Your sublimation printer has no white ink at all, so a white background and a transparent background do exactly the same thing.

Export transparent anyway — it is the only way you can see which parts of the design are windows. Open the PNG on a dark background and every window lights up.

Share → Download → PNG → Transparent background ON. Tick 2× size if it is offered. (Transparent background is Canva Pro, Teams, Business, Education and Nonprofits only.)

Mirror once. Most printer software mirrors for you — do both and it is not mirrored at all.

Canva works in RGB and converts automatically, so press a colour swatch sheet once onto your actual blanks and keep it. Your own swatch beats any screen.

Seven checks before you press

  • The background is deleted, not white
  • Everything that must be visible is INK, not white
  • You looked at it on a mid-tone background and it still reads
  • The PNG is transparent, and in the thousands of pixels
  • The blank is polyester or polymer coated — and light
  • You mirrored exactly once. Printer software OR Canva, not both
  • Every element came from a Canva search filtered to Free

The first three are all the same thing, which tells you how often it is the thing that goes wrong.
