The one that closes shops

Pro content may NOT go on a t-shirt you sell. From Canva's Content Licence Agreement, in the list of things you may not do with Pro Content:

Use or display the Content on a standalone basis on websites or other venues designed to induce or involving the sale, license or other distribution of 'on demand' products, including postcards, mugs, t-shirts, posters and other items.

Free content explicitly may. Same agreement, permitted uses of Free Content:

prints, posters (i.e. hardcopies) and other reproductions for personal or promotional purposes, resale, license or other distribution.

So the whole licence turns on one small crown in the corner of a thumbnail — and once the element is on your canvas you cannot see it at all. There is no "is this safe to sell" button.

So the check happens at the point of placing

  • Elements → the filter icon → Price: Free. Every search, not once per session — it does not always persist.
  • Unsure about an element already in a design? Delete it and place it again from a filtered search.
  • On a free plan the download dialog lists Pro content before it will export. On Pro it says nothing, because for every other purpose you are entitled to use it. Your subscription is not the issue — the licence for this specific use is.

Rebuilding is cheaper than a takedown.

The print file

  • Print area (max) — 12 × 16 in
  • Resolution — 150–300 DPI
  • Pixels across — 3,600–4,800
  • Format — PNG, transparent

4500 × 5400 px is a comfortable middle that fits a 12 × 16 area at 300 DPI.

Those are Printful's figures, named as an example. Providers differ — check your own provider's spec page before you commit to a number.

The export, and the irony in it

Canva's Help Centre is explicit that downloading with a transparent background is "only available to Canva Pro, Canva Teams, Canva Business, Canva Education, and Canva for Nonprofits plans".

So you need Pro for the export, and you must not use Pro in the design. Both are true at once. A free plan can design a legal shirt and cannot export it properly; a Pro plan can export it properly and can very easily make it illegal.

Share → Download → PNG → Transparent background ON. Tick 2× size if it is offered — more pixels is the cheap win.

Six checks before you upload

  • Every element came from a search filtered to Free, or you made it.
  • PNG with transparency actually on — checked against a dark background.
  • Dimensions in the thousands, matching your provider's print area.
  • Your thinnest line survives at 100% zoom. Fabric cannot hold a hairline.
  • Print file and listing images in different folders.
  • A note of where every element came from. Two lines per design.

The first one is the one that matters legally, and the one that cannot be fixed after the fact.
