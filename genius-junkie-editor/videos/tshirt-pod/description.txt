Canva's own licence agreement names t-shirts in the list of things you may NOT
do with Pro content. Free elements are explicitly allowed for resale. So the
whole licence turns on one small crown icon in the corner of a thumbnail — and
Canva mixes free and Pro results in the same element search.

And the part nobody says out loud: the transparent PNG that print on demand
requires is itself a Pro feature. You need Pro for the export, and you must not
use Pro in the design.

This is the whole build, done to Canva's published licence terms.

THE RULES, FROM CANVA'S OWN PAGES
· Content Licence Agreement, prohibited uses of Pro Content: "…'on demand'
  products, including postcards, mugs, t-shirts, posters and other items."
· Same agreement, permitted uses of Free Content: "prints, posters (i.e.
  hardcopies) and other reproductions for… resale."
· Canva Help Centre: downloading with a transparent background is "only
  available to Canva Pro, Canva Teams, Canva Business, Canva Education, and
  Canva for Nonprofits plans."
Verified 5 October 2026.

THE PRINT FILE — PRINTFUL'S FIGURES, AS AN EXAMPLE
· Maximum t-shirt print area 12 × 16 in
· 150–300 DPI, which is roughly 3,600–4,800 px across
· PNG or JPEG
4500 × 5400 px is a comfortable middle that fits a 12 × 16 area at 300 DPI.
Providers differ — check your own provider's spec page before you commit.
