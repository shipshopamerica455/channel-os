# How to Fix Mobile View on a Canva Website

## Facts verified 2026-11-06

- **THE TRAP — there is no separate phone layout to edit; the phone version is
  REFLOWED from your one design.** Canva Help Centre, "My website looks
  different on mobile" (canva.com/help/responsive-website-issues): *"Reflow
  adapts Canva websites to different screen sizes by stacking elements
  vertically."* On mobile view, overlapping elements shrink and ungrouped
  elements can separate — grouping keeps them together: *"Otherwise, they might
  separate in mobile view."* Canva's help pages describe a mobile **preview**,
  not a separate mobile layout (stated on screen as "the help pages describe a
  preview", not as "there is no mobile editor").
- **CANVA'S FIVE FIXES**, same page, verbatim: *"Select elements in your design
  and resize or crop them so their borders don't touch."* · *"Replace large
  elements that span the full width with smaller ones that stack neatly."* ·
  *"Keep important elements inside the safe margin boundaries."* · *"Use the
  mobile preview panel while editing."* — *"Treat it as an approximation, since
  the published site can vary by device and browser."* · *"Group related
  elements, then lock the group so they stay together."*
- **PATHS.** Group: select the elements (drag across them, or hold Shift and
  click each) → floating toolbar → **Group**. Mobile check: **Preview** → in the
  preview panel, the **Mobile** button. Canva Help "Publish Canva websites":
  the Mobile Preview shows the phone view live while you edit, and *"elements
  displayed side by side on desktop may stack vertically on mobile."*
- **RESIZE ON MOBILE.** Canva Help "Publish websites to a free domain": *"Turn
  on Resize on mobile to optimize your site for mobile devices."* Steps:
  Publish website → Site address → Settings → Continue → Publish. Canva Help
  "Designing websites": *"Resize on mobile: Automatically adjust your design for
  mobile screens."*
- **LIMITS** (Canva Help "Designing websites"): up to 45 pages; up to 20 sections
  per page while designing; Canva Free publishes up to **10 sections per page**,
  paid plans up to 20.

NOT CLAIMED: a phone canvas size — no Canva page gives one, and the
"512 × 1042" figure on the outlier is a viewer comment, so the script says
exactly that; what a site looks like with Resize on mobile switched OFF (not
documented, not stated); any plan price.

## S01 — HOOK (~120 words)

> If your Canva website looks broken on a phone, you are probably trying to fix
> it in the wrong place.
>
> There is no second, phone-sized design hiding in there. Canva's help pages
> describe a mobile preview — and the phone version is built from your one
> design, by stacking everything vertically. Canva calls it reflow.
>
> And reflow has two rules that cause almost every broken page. Elements that
> overlap get shrunk. Elements that are not grouped can get pulled apart.
>
> So the fix is not on the phone. It is in the desktop design: stop things
> touching, and group the things that belong together.
>
> There is a free mobile check card in the description. Let me break a site on
> purpose, then fix it properly.

## S02 — THE FREE CARD, AND THREE PRINCIPLES (~115 words)

> The free card is linked below — Canva's reflow rules, the five fixes, and a
> page-by-page check, on one sheet.
>
> Three principles.
>
> One: there is one design. Whatever you see on the phone was made from the
> desktop layout, so that is where every fix goes.
>
> Two: the phone stacks. Anything side by side on a wide screen becomes a
> column on a narrow one, and the order of that column is the order you built
> it in.
>
> Three: the preview is a guide, not a guarantee. Canva says so itself. The
> only test that counts is the published site on a real phone.
>
> One design, it stacks, test the real thing.

## S03 — WHAT A PHONE ACTUALLY DOES TO YOUR SITE (~150 words)

> Start with what is really happening, because once you can see it, every fix
> makes sense.
>
> Here is a desktop page. A heading, a photo beside it, three little boxes in a
> row underneath, and a button. On a laptop, it is wide, so things sit side by
> side.
>
> A phone is narrow. Canva's help says reflow adapts the site by stacking
> elements vertically — so the heading goes on top, the photo underneath it,
> then the three boxes, one after another, then the button.
>
> When everything is separate and nothing touches, that is fine. That is a
> perfectly good phone page.
>
> The trouble starts with the two cases reflow handles badly. Things that
> overlap, like words placed over a photo. And things that only look connected,
> like a price sitting next to a picture, but were never actually grouped.
>
> Keep those two in your head. They are the whole video.

## S04 — OPENING THE MOBILE PREVIEW (~140 words)

> So how do you see it before a guest or a customer does?
>
> Select Preview. In the preview panel there is a Mobile button — press it,
> and you get the stacked phone version of the page you are working on.
>
> Canva's publishing help adds something useful: the mobile preview updates
> live while you edit. So keep it open next to your design. Move something on
> the desktop page, and you watch the phone version change.
>
> One warning, in Canva's own words. Treat the preview as an approximation,
> because the published site can vary by device and browser. It is very good
> for catching the big problems. It is not proof.
>
> And a number people go looking for: the size of the phone page. Canva's help
> does not give one. If you have seen a figure quoted, that came from a comment,
> not from Canva.

## S05 — THE CHECK, PAGE BY PAGE (~160 words)

> Now the check itself, and it is the same three passes on every page.
>
> Pass one, the order. Read the phone version from the top, like a stranger.
> Does it still make sense? The heading should come before the photo, the
> photo before the details, and the button last. If the order reads wrong, it
> is because of where things sit on the desktop page.
>
> Pass two, the size. Look for text that has suddenly gone tiny. A heading that
> was bold on the laptop and is now a line of crumbs on the phone is almost
> always overlapping something.
>
> Pass three, the gaps. Look for things that have drifted apart — a label three
> blocks away from the picture it describes, a price under the wrong product.
> That is the ungrouped case.
>
> Order, size, gaps. Write down what you find on each page before you fix
> anything. On this one I have found two problems. Let me show you exactly why
> they happen.

## S06 — THE TRAP, DEMONSTRATED (~190 words)

> So here is the broken page, and I am going to do what almost everyone does
> first.
>
> The heading is tiny on the phone. So I go to the phone preview and try to
> drag it bigger. Nothing — the preview is a preview. I can look, but the phone
> version is not something I can arrange by hand.
>
> So back to the desktop page, and look at why.
>
> The heading is sitting on top of the photo. It overlaps. Canva's rule is
> that overlapping elements shrink on mobile — so the phone squeezed my heading
> to fit inside the photo, and it came out the size of a footnote.
>
> Second problem. The price for the candle. On the laptop it sits right beside
> the picture. On the phone, it is three blocks further down, under a different
> product. They were never grouped. They only looked like they belonged
> together, and reflow pulled them apart.
>
> Now the fix, both of them, on the desktop page. Move the heading off the
> photo, so their borders do not touch. Select the picture and its price, and
> group them.
>
> Back to the preview. The heading is full size. The price is under its candle.
> Nothing was fixed on the phone.

## S07 — FIX ONE: STOP THINGS TOUCHING (~140 words)

> Fix one, in Canva's words: resize or crop elements so their borders don't
> touch.
>
> The hard part is seeing what touches, because a lot of elements are bigger
> than they look. A photo with a soft fade still has a full rectangle. A text
> box can stretch far past the last word. Click each one and look at its box,
> not its picture.
>
> Then give each one its own space. Shorten the text box so it stops where the
> words stop. Crop the photo so it ends before the heading begins. Move the
> decorative shape so it sits behind nothing.
>
> And words over a photo are the classic case. It looks lovely on a laptop, and
> it is exactly what shrinks on a phone. Put the heading above or beside the
> photo instead, and check the preview after each move.

## S08 — FIX TWO: GROUP, THEN LOCK (~140 words)

> Fix two is for things that belong together. A product and its price. An icon
> and its label. A photo and its caption.
>
> Select them — drag across them, or hold Shift and click each one. On the
> floating toolbar, choose Group. Now reflow moves them as one block, and they
> arrive on the phone together.
>
> Then lock the group. Canva's help says group related elements and lock the
> group, so they stay together. The lock stops you knocking them apart later
> while you are tidying something else.
>
> Check it straight away. Open the mobile preview, and the product and its price
> should now arrive on the phone side by side, or one directly under the other —
> never with anything in between.
>
> One caution. Grouping is for things that belong together, not for making a
> whole page one giant block. A group the full width of the page is the third
> problem, and that is next.

## S09 — FIX THREE: NOTHING FULL WIDTH, EVERYTHING INSIDE (~140 words)

> Fix three is two of Canva's rules that go together.
>
> First, replace large elements that span the full width with smaller ones that
> stack neatly. A row of three boxes made as one wide picture cannot stack — it
> is one thing, so the phone just shrinks it. Rebuild it as three separate
> boxes, and the phone turns them into a tidy column of three.
>
> Second, keep important elements inside the safe margin. Anything right up
> against the edge of the page is the first thing to get clipped or squeezed on
> a narrow screen. Pull your text and buttons in from the edges.
>
> And the button gets one more check of its own. On the phone it has to be big
> enough to hit with a thumb. If it looks small in the preview, make it bigger
> on the desktop page.

## S10 — PUBLISH, THEN TEST THE REAL THING (~140 words)

> Then publish, and do the test that actually counts.
>
> Publish website. Site address. Settings — and in Settings, turn on Resize on
> mobile. Canva's help says it automatically adjusts the design for mobile
> screens. Then Continue, and Publish.
>
> Now open the published address on a real phone. Not the preview — the live
> site, in the phone's own browser. Go through every page, top to bottom, with
> the same three passes. Order, size, gaps.
>
> Then do it on a second phone if you can, a different make. Canva says the
> published site can vary by device and browser, and the only way to know is to
> look.
>
> If something is wrong, you know where the fix goes now. Back to the desktop
> design, change it, publish again. The same address shows the new version, so
> nobody needs a new link.

## S11 — BUILD IT MOBILE-SAFE FROM THE START (~130 words)

> If you make more than one of these, for yourself, for clients, or as templates
> to sell, stop fixing and start building it right the first time.
>
> Three habits. Never put words on top of a photo. Group every pair that belongs
> together the moment you make it, and lock it. And build rows from separate
> pieces, never one wide picture.
>
> Two limits are worth knowing while you build. A Canva website can have up to
> forty-five pages. And on a free account you can publish up to ten sections per
> page — you can design with up to twenty, but the free plan publishes ten.
>
> So plan the sections before you design them, and if a page needs more than
> ten, split it into two pages instead.
>
> Keep the mobile preview open the whole time, and the check at the end takes
> two minutes instead of an evening.

## S12 — THE CHECKS (~110 words)

> Before you send the address to anyone.
>
> Nothing overlaps — every element has its own space. Words are never on top of a
> photo. Every pair that belongs together is grouped and locked. No single
> element spans the full width. Text and buttons sit inside the safe margin. The
> button is big enough for a thumb. Resize on mobile is on. And the published
> site has been checked on a real phone, every page, top to bottom.
>
> Eight checks. The last one is the only one that tells you the truth — the
> other seven are how you get there first time, instead of on the third try,
> after a customer has already sent you a screenshot.
