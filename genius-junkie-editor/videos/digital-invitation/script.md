# How to Make a Clickable Digital Invitation in Canva (2027)

## Facts verified 2026-10-28

- **THE TRAP — the tap dies in the file.** Canva Help Centre, "Add hyperlinks"
  (en_gb, read 28 Oct 2026): *"Hyperlinks will not work on PNGs, JPGs, MP4s,
  etc., or when you post your design on social media."* *"Links remain clickable
  by downloading your design as a PDF or by presenting it."* *"Hyperlinks will
  only work if you download your design as a PDF (except flattened PDFs)."* *"For
  videos, hyperlinks are only clickable in the Presenter window while on
  Presenter view."* *"Image formats like JPG and PNG do not support hyperlinks
  regardless of the image source."*
  Saving the invitation as an image or a video and dropping it into a group chat
  is the usual way a digital invitation gets sent — and it is exactly the case
  Canva says kills every link. The envelope looks tappable and does nothing.
- **PAGE LINKS.** Same page: *"To link to another page within the design, select
  the page you want to link to from the Pages in this document section in the
  dropdown."*
- **HOW A WEBSITE PUBLISHES.** Canva Help Centre, "Design websites": *"When
  published, each page will appear as a separate scrollable page on the same
  website."* Links: select the element → link → *Pages in this document*. Up to
  20 sections per page while designing, **Canva Free publishes 10 per page**.
  *Resize on mobile* adjusts the design for mobile screens; a design not made
  from a website template is asked to resize before publishing.
- **THE FREE ADDRESS.** Canva Help Centre, "Publish websites to a free domain"
  (re-checked 28 Oct 2026): the free domain steps apply to designs created from a
  **Website template only**; *"Canva Free users can have up to five live websites
  on the free domain"*; link-in-bio and mobile-site designs publish to a
  `canva.link` address only. The *Designed with Canva* footer stays on Free
  (verified 19 Oct for the wedding video).
- **ANIMATION.** Canva Help: Animate applies to a page, text or elements. How an
  animation behaves on a PUBLISHED site is not stated — so the script says
  "check it in Preview", never "it plays on the site".

NOT CLAIMED: the exact free-domain suffix (the help page and the Terms of Use
word it differently), whether a public view link keeps links clickable (not
stated by Canva), how any messaging app previews a link, the price of any plan
or domain, and whether page transitions play on a published site.

## S01 — HOOK (~120 words)

> Most clickable digital invitations stop being clickable the moment you send
> them.
>
> You tap the envelope, and nothing opens. Canva's own help page says why:
> hyperlinks will not work on PNGs, JPGs, or MP4s — or when you post your design
> on social media.
>
> And saving the invitation as a picture, or a video, and dropping it into the
> group chat is exactly how most people send one. So every guest gets a lovely
> envelope that does nothing.
>
> The fix is to build it as a small website, link the envelope to the next page,
> publish it on Canva's free address, and send the link — not the picture.
>
> There is a free send card in the description. Let me build one properly.

## S02 — THE FREE CARD, AND THREE PRINCIPLES (~115 words)

> The free card is linked below — which ways of sending keep the tap working,
> which ones kill it, and the checks, on one page.
>
> Three principles.
>
> One: send the link, not the file. A picture of an invitation is a picture, no
> matter how clickable it looked in Canva.
>
> Two: one page per moment. The envelope, then the invitation, then the details.
> Each tap should move the guest one step.
>
> Three: test it the way a guest meets it — on a phone, from a message — not in
> the editor, where everything works.
>
> You can build all of this on a free account — Canva's help says websites are
> available on Canva Free. Just check the label on any element you use, because
> some of them are Pro.
>
> Send the link, one page per moment, test it on a phone.

## S03 — START FROM A WEBSITE (~150 words)

> Start from the right kind of design, because this decides everything later.
>
> In Canva, create a design and search for website. Pick a website template, or
> a blank website. Not a poster, not an Instagram post, and not a mobile site or
> link in bio — Canva's help page says those publish to a canva dot link
> address only, and the free web address needs a design made from a website
> template.
>
> Now make three pages. Page one is the envelope. Page two is the invitation
> itself — what it is, when, and where. Page three is the details: the map, the
> reply button, anything a guest needs to look up twice.
>
> When it is published, Canva's help says each page appears as a separate
> scrollable page on the same website. That is what lets the envelope open:
> one page closed, the next page open.

## S04 — THE ENVELOPE (~150 words)

> Page one, the envelope.
>
> Set a background colour — a soft one, so the envelope stands out. In Elements,
> search envelope and drag one onto the page. Make it big. On a phone, this is
> the only thing on the screen, and it has to look like something you can open.
>
> Add a seal in the middle — a circle, with the first letter of the name on it.
> It reads instantly as a closed letter.
>
> Then the line that does the real work: you are invited — tap to open. Put it
> under the envelope, large, in plain words. A guest should never have to guess
> that the picture does something.
>
> One more thing. Keep the envelope and the words as two separate pieces, not
> grouped into one picture. You are going to link both of them, so a tap
> anywhere near the envelope opens it.

## S05 — MAKE IT OPEN (~165 words)

> Now the part that makes it an invitation instead of a picture.
>
> Select the envelope. In the toolbar, open More, then Link. At the bottom of
> the dropdown you will see a section called Pages in this document. Choose page
> two.
>
> Now select the words — tap to open — and do the same. Link, Pages in this
> document, page two. Two targets, one destination.
>
> Page two is the opened card. Put the card in the same spot the envelope was,
> so it feels like the envelope opened rather than the page changed. The name of
> the event at the top. Then the date, the time, and the place, each on its own
> line, big enough to read on a phone without zooming.
>
> If you want movement, select the card and use Animate. Then check it in
> Preview before you trust it. Preview is the closest you get to what a guest
> will see.
>
> At the bottom of page two, one more link: see the details, linked to page
> three.

## S06 — THE TRAP, DEMONSTRATED (~210 words)

> Here is the invitation most people send, so you can see exactly where it goes
> wrong.
>
> Same design. Download, PNG. Into the group chat. A guest opens it, taps the
> envelope, and nothing happens. Taps the words, tap to open. Nothing.
>
> Try the fancier version. Download as an MP4, with the envelope animating open.
> It plays, it looks great, and the reply button at the end cannot be tapped.
>
> Canva's help page says this in so many words. Hyperlinks will not work on
> PNGs, JPGs, MP4s, et cetera, or when you post your design on social media.
> Image formats like JPG and PNG do not support hyperlinks, regardless of the
> image source. And for videos, hyperlinks are only clickable in the presenter
> window, while you are presenting.
>
> So a picture of an invitation is a picture, and a video of one is a video.
>
> Now the same design, published as a website, and sent as a link. Tap the link.
> The envelope fills the screen. Tap the envelope, and the card opens. Tap see
> the details, and there is the map and the reply button, and both of them work.
>
> Same design, same pages, same links. The only thing that changed is what you
> sent.

## S07 — THE DETAILS PAGE (~150 words)

> Page three is the page guests come back to, so make it easy to scan.
>
> The address, written out in full, with a button under it that links to a map.
> Find the place in your maps app, copy its share link, and paste that onto the
> button with Link. Now a guest taps once and gets directions.
>
> Then the reply button. Link it to a form, or to an email address, or to a
> message. If you build invitations for other people, make the form in their
> account, not yours — whoever makes a form owns every reply. There is a whole
> video on that trap, linked at the end.
>
> Then anything else a guest will look up twice: the dress code, the parking, a
> gift list link.
>
> Keep every button the same size and the same colour, so a guest learns once
> what a button looks like here.

## S08 — BUILT FOR THE PHONE (~140 words)

> Almost every guest will open this on a phone, so check it as a phone.
>
> When you publish, look in the settings for Resize on mobile and turn it on.
> Canva says it adjusts your design for mobile screens.
>
> Then do not trust it. Preview the site in mobile view, and go through every
> page, top to bottom.
>
> You are looking for three things. Text that has shrunk too small to read.
> Buttons too small to tap with a thumb. And anything that sat side by side on
> the desktop, which now has to stack — that is where things move.
>
> A good test: every line of text on the envelope and the card should be
> readable with the phone at arm's length. If you have to squint in Preview,
> a guest will have to squint on the bus.

## S09 — PUBLISH IT FREE (~150 words)

> Now publish.
>
> Select Publish website. Choose a site address — keep it short, because people
> will see it in the message. Open the settings, turn on Resize on mobile, then
> Continue, and Publish.
>
> Two things to know before you do.
>
> First, on a free account, Canva's help page says you can have up to five live
> websites on the free domain. An invitation is only needed until the event,
> so take it down afterwards and the slot is yours again.
>
> Second, Canva Free publishes up to ten sections per page. An invitation
> should never come close, but if a page is missing something on the published
> site, that limit is the first thing to check.
>
> Then open the published link on your own phone, from a message to yourself,
> and tap through all three pages. That is the version your guests will get.

## S10 — SENDING IT (~140 words)

> Sending is where most of these break, so do it on purpose.
>
> Paste the link into the message, with one line of words in front of it. Your
> invitation to Maya's thirtieth — tap the link to open it. A bare web address
> on its own looks like spam. A sentence tells people it is meant for them.
>
> If someone needs a file instead — an email to an older relative, or a printer
> — download a PDF. Canva says links remain clickable in a PDF, except a
> flattened PDF. So choose the standard PDF, not the flattened one, and the
> envelope still opens.
>
> What you never send is the PNG or the MP4 on their own. If you want something
> pretty in the chat, send the picture as a teaser, and the link underneath it.
> The picture gets the attention. The link gets the reply.

## S11 — ONE BUILD, EVERY EVENT (~130 words)

> Once one invitation works, do not start the next one from scratch.
>
> Duplicate the design. Change the name on the seal, the words on the card, and
> the details page. The pages, the links and the buttons all come with it, and
> they already work.
>
> Check the links after you duplicate, though. A link on the envelope that
> points at page two still points at page two. But if you add or remove a page,
> re-link anything that moved.
>
> And when an event is over, unpublish its site. With five live sites on a free
> account, a birthday from March should not be taking the slot that December's
> party needs.
>
> One build, a new site for each event, taken down when it is done.

## S12 — THE CHECKS (~120 words)

> Before you send it to a single guest, seven checks.
>
> One. It was made from a website template.
>
> Two. The envelope and the words are both linked to page two.
>
> Three. Page two links to the details.
>
> Four. Every button on the details page goes where it says.
>
> Five. You opened the published link on a phone, from a message, and tapped
> through every page.
>
> Six. The text is readable at arm's length.
>
> Seven. You are sending the link — not the PNG, not the MP4.
>
> The fifth is the one nobody does, because everything works in the editor. The
> editor is not where your guests are.
