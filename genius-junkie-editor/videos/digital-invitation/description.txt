Most clickable digital invitations stop being clickable the moment you send
them — you tap the envelope in the group chat, and nothing opens.

Canva's own help page says why: "Hyperlinks will not work on PNGs, JPGs, MP4s,
etc., or when you post your design on social media." And saving the invitation
as a picture or a video and dropping it into the chat is exactly how most people
send one.

This builds it the way that keeps the tap working: a small free Canva website —
an envelope page, the opened card, a details page — with the envelope linked to
the next page, published on Canva's free address, and sent as a LINK.

WHERE THE TAP SURVIVES (Canva Help Centre, "Add hyperlinks")
· "Links remain clickable by downloading your design as a PDF or by presenting it."
· "Hyperlinks will only work if you download your design as a PDF (except
  flattened PDFs)."
· "Hyperlinks will not work on PNGs, JPGs, MP4s, etc., or when you post your
  design on social media."
· "For videos, hyperlinks are only clickable in the Presenter window while on
  Presenter view."

CANVA WEBSITES (Canva Help Centre)
· Each page publishes as "a separate scrollable page on the same website".
· Link a page under Link → "Pages in this document".
· The free web address needs a design made from a WEBSITE template; mobile-site
  and link-in-bio designs publish to a canva.link address only.
· "Canva Free users can have up to five live websites on the free domain."
· Canva Free publishes up to 10 sections per page.
