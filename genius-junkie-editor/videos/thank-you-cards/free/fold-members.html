The one that gets everyone

Rotate the LEFT panel 180° on the sheet.

Fold a sheet in half and the top half flips over — so the left panel becomes the back of the card, upside down. Anything on it has to be placed upside down on the sheet to come out the right way up on the card.

The INSIDE (page 2) is never rotated. It reads as one spread, left to right, exactly as it looks on screen. Only the panel that folds behind changes orientation.

One rotated panel. Outside. Left. That is the entire rule.

30-second test: scrap paper, write FRONT bottom-right and BACK bottom-left, fold it, and look at where BACK ended up.

Sizes

  • Card (finished) — 105 × 148 mm (A6) · US: 5.5 × 4.25 in
  • Sheet you print — 210 × 148 mm (A5 landscape) · US: 8.5 × 5.5 in
  • Margin, all round — 10 mm · 0.4 in
  • Fold guide — 105 mm, the exact centre · 4.25 in

Printing

  • Export PDF Print (300 DPI). PDF Standard is 96 DPI and this has type on it.
  • Double-sided: your printer says “flip on long edge” or “flip on short edge”, and the names differ by make.
  • Do not guess it. Print one, fold it, look at it. Wrong way up? Change the flip edge and print one more.
  • Write the printer and the setting that worked on the back of that test card, in pencil. Keep it in the drawer with the paper.
  • Four up: four A6 fronts on an A4 sheet, artwork touching on the shared line, then cut down it.
  • Steel rule and a sharp blade, three light passes. Scissors wander.

The fold

Score before you fold. Run the back of a butter knife — or a bone folder — down the centre line against a ruler. You are crushing the fibres in a straight line, not cutting them. Unscored card stock cracks along the spine and you get a white line through the fold. Once it cracks, that card is finished.
