# How to Make Certificates in Canva with Bulk Create (Fifty Names, One Click)

## Facts verified 2026-08-18 — all from Canva's own Help Centre

- **How it works:** "Each column in your data acts as a single, distinct data
  field… Each row in your data contains the information to generate one
  complete page or design." Source: Help Centre, "Create designs in bulk".
- **You must build placeholders FIRST:** "it's essential to prepare your design
  template with placeholder elements before you connect your data… Ensure you
  have a **separate text box for each data field**." Images need frames or grids.
  Same source.
- **Multi-page templates:** "You will need to set up the data connections on
  each page of your template." Same source.
- **Limits:** up to **150 data fields**, and from a Canva Sheet "you can select
  up to **300 rows** and 150 columns only." Same source.
- **THE TRAP — Advanced options.** It sits *below* the Continue button, so
  almost nobody opens it. Output defaults to **"1 design with multiple pages"**.
  The other choice is **"Individual designs"**, which creates a separate design
  file per row, "automatically grouped into a new folder in your Projects". And
  **"Name each page using"** names every page or file after a column of your
  data. Same source. Miss it and you get one long design and fifty identically
  named files.
- **Availability:** Bulk Create is "available to Canva Pro, Canva Teams, Canva
  Business, Canva for Enterprise, Canva Education, and Canva for Nonprofits
  users **on desktop only**." Same source.
- **But free for teachers:** Canva for Education is free for verified K-12
  teachers and includes the Pro feature set, so Bulk Create is included. Verify
  with a school email at canva.com/education; verification typically takes
  24–48 hours.
- **Data sources:** a Canva Sheet, an uploaded CSV or XLSX, a connected data
  app, or typed in manually. Same source.
- **Connecting:** "Auto-match fields" uses AI, or drag a field onto an element
  manually. The preview is review-only — "To make changes, go back to the data
  connection step." Same source.
- **Image trap:** "image URLs from cloud photo storage are not supported and
  will be treated as plain text." Images must be embedded inside a single cell.
  Same source.

Target: ~1,950 words @ 3.70 w/s ≈ 8 min 47 s. Ad breaks after S05 and S09.

## S01 — HOOK (~125 words)

> You line up fifty names, you run Bulk Create, and Canva does exactly what you
> asked. One design. Fifty pages. And then you go to send them out and you
> realise you have one enormous file, and when you split it you get fifty PDFs
> called certificate one through certificate fifty, with no names on them, and
> the only way to find out whose is whose is to open all fifty. There is a
> setting that fixes this completely. It takes one click. It is hidden
> underneath the Continue button, which is why almost nobody finds it. I will
> show you exactly where. But first we are going to design the certificate,
> because Bulk Create only works if you build it the right way from the start.

## S02 — THE HONEST BIT, FIRST (~145 words)

> Before you spend twenty minutes on this, one thing you need to know, because
> most tutorials skip it. Bulk Create is not on the free plan. It is a Pro
> feature, and it is desktop only, so it will not appear on your phone at all.
> That is straight from Canva's own documentation. But here is the part that
> matters if you are a teacher, and it is the reason I have put this second
> instead of at the end. Canva for Education is free for verified K to twelve
> teachers, and it includes the whole Pro feature set, which includes Bulk
> Create. You verify with a school email address at canva dot com slash
> education and it usually takes a day or two. So if you are making certificates
> for a classroom, this is free. Genuinely free, not trial free.

## S03 — HOW BULK CREATE ACTUALLY THINKS (~155 words)

> Two sentences and the whole feature makes sense. Each column in your data is
> one field, like first name, or course, or date. And each row is one finished
> page. Fifty rows, fifty certificates. That is it. Which means the work is not
> in the data, it is in the template, and this is where people go wrong before
> they even open the tool. Canva connects a data field to an element on your
> page. One field, one element. So every single thing that changes between
> certificates has to be its own separate text box. If you type "Awarded to
> blank" as one text box, you cannot connect just the blank half. Canva has no
> way to reach inside it. You would have to replace the whole line, including
> the words "Awarded to". Separate boxes. Set that up first and everything after
> it is easy.

## S04 — DESIGNING THE CERTIFICATE (~150 words)

> New design, and search Certificate. Landscape A4 is the safe choice, because
> it prints on ordinary paper anywhere in the world and it fits an email. Now
> the layout, and there are only four things on a certificate that matter. The
> award line at the top, which is fixed and never changes. The recipient's name,
> which is the biggest thing on the page and the only thing anyone actually
> looks at. The reason, one line, underneath. And the date and signature at the
> bottom. Everything else is decoration. Set the name at somewhere around eighty
> to a hundred point, because a certificate is looked at from arm's length on a
> wall, not read on a screen. And leave real space around it. The most common
> way a certificate looks cheap is that everything is crammed into the middle
> with a border squeezing it.

## S05 — BUILDING THE PLACEHOLDERS PROPERLY (~195 words)

> Now the part that decides whether this works. Every field gets its own text
> box. Click the name, and check it really is on its own, not sharing a box with
> the line above. Type a placeholder into it that you will recognise later, so
> literally the word NAME in capitals, because in a minute you are going to be
> looking for it in a list. Same for the course, so type COURSE. Same for the
> date, type DATE. Three separate boxes, three obvious placeholders. And style
> each one now, before you connect anything, because whatever you set here is
> what all fifty inherit. Font, size, colour, alignment. Especially alignment.
> Set the name box to centred, and set it as a fixed width box rather than one
> that grows, because you have got names in that list that are four letters long
> and names that are twenty five, and if the box grows from the left, the short
> ones will sit off to one side and the long ones will run off the edge. Centred,
> fixed width. That one setting is the difference between fifty tidy
> certificates and fifty that need fixing by hand.

## S06 — THE SETTING NOBODY FINDS (~205 words)

> Right. The thing I promised. When your data is connected, there is a big
> Continue button, and underneath it, in small text, there is Advanced options.
> Nobody clicks it. It looks like the kind of thing you would only need if you
> were doing something unusual, and it is the single most useful part of the
> whole feature. Open it and you get three settings. The first is Output, and it
> has two choices. One design with multiple pages, which is the default, and
> that is the one that gives you a single fifty page file. Or, Individual
> designs, which creates a separate Canva design for every row and drops them
> all into their own folder in your Projects. That is the one you want for
> certificates, because a certificate is something you send to one person. The
> second setting is the one that genuinely saves an hour. Name each page using.
> Open that dropdown and pick a column from your data. Pick the name column.
> Now every single file is named after the person it belongs to. Not certificate
> forty one. Their actual name. And the third is Save in, so you can send the
> whole lot straight into a folder instead of into the middle of your projects.

## S07 — THE DATA (~175 words)

> Now the list. You have got four ways in and you only need one. If it is
> already a spreadsheet, save it as a C S V or an X L S X and upload it. If you
> have not got one, the easiest route is Enter data manually, which builds the
> table right inside Canva. Click Clear table first, because it comes with
> sample data in it and if you leave that, you will generate certificates for
> people who do not exist. Then add a column per field. Now, two limits worth
> knowing before you plan a big run. You can connect up to a hundred and fifty
> data fields, which nobody will ever hit. And from a Canva Sheet you can select
> up to three hundred rows. If you have got a whole school to do, that is your
> ceiling per run, so split it into batches by year group, which you probably
> wanted anyway. Check your column headings are clean before you upload. The
> headings become the field names you drag, so First name is a lot easier to
> work with than Column D.

## S08 — CONNECTING, AND CHECKING (~165 words)

> With the data in, you connect fields to elements, and there are two ways.
> Auto-match fields lets Canva work it out with A I. It analyses the design and
> matches your columns to your text boxes, and it is genuinely good, especially
> if you named your placeholders sensibly like I told you to. Or you drag each
> field from the side panel and drop it onto the element yourself. Either way,
> and this is the important part, check it. Auto-match can put the date where
> the course should be, particularly if two of your columns look similar. So
> read the connections in the side panel before you go on. Then preview. And
> know what the preview is and is not. It is review only. You cannot edit
> anything in it. If something is wrong, you go back to the data connection step,
> fix it there, and preview again. People lose time trying to type into the
> preview and wondering why nothing happens.

## S09 — PUTTING PHOTOS AND LOGOS IN (~155 words)

> If you want something different on each certificate that is not text, a school
> crest, a headshot, a badge, that works too, and it has one rule that catches
> everybody. Images do not go in as links. You add a frame or a grid from
> Elements onto your template, that is the placeholder, and then in your data
> your image column has to contain the image itself, embedded in the cell. Not a
> Google Drive link, not a Dropbox link. Canva's documentation says it plainly.
> Image U R Ls from cloud photo storage are not supported and will be treated as
> plain text, which means instead of a photo you get the words http colon slash
> slash printed across your certificate. Embed the picture in the cell, one
> image per cell, not floating across several. Then drag the image field onto
> the frame exactly like you did with the text.

**→ AD BREAK 2** (1.4 s scripted silence + hard cut card, after S09)

## S10 — GENERATING, AND WHAT YOU GET (~150 words)

> Everything connected, Advanced options set the way we just did, and the button
> at the bottom tells you what is about to happen. Create fifty designs. Click
> it. What you get, if you set Output to Individual designs, is a folder in your
> Projects with fifty separate Canva files, each one named after the person on
> it. And they are real Canva designs, not flat images, so if one name was
> spelled wrong you open that one file and fix that one certificate, rather than
> hunting through a fifty page document. One more thing. If your template had
> more than one page, front and back for example, you have to set the data
> connections up on every page. Canva does not carry them across for you. That
> catches people with two sided certificates and it is a quiet one, because the
> front generates perfectly and the back comes out blank.

## S11 — SENDING THEM OUT (~145 words)

> Last step. If you are printing them yourself, download as P D F Print, because
> that is three hundred D P I with proper colour, and a certificate is a print
> object. If you are emailing them, P D F Standard is ninety six D P I and much
> lighter, and it will look the same on a screen. If you are putting them on a
> website or a school newsletter, P N G. And here is where that Advanced option
> pays you back, because you are not renaming anything. Every file already has
> the right name on it, so attaching the right certificate to the right email is
> a matter of reading the filename, not opening the file. On a run of fifty that
> is the difference between five minutes and an hour, and it is the entire
> reason I made you scroll down to that little link.
