The Photo Album Kit — the fold already accounted for, on every page

A photo album is the one design where people put their best photo right across the middle of two pages. It is also the one design where that is the worst place to put it — and the reason is printed on Canva’s own product page, where almost nobody reads it.

Canva’s own photo books page, twice

Asked whether they offer layflat binding, Canva answer: “No, we don’t offer layflat binding at this time. We do, however, offer PUR binding using a strong bookbinding adhesive.”

PUR is a good adhesive. It opens flatter than ordinary perfect binding and the book will not fall apart. But it is not layflat, so the centre of every spread curves down into the spine. Put a face, a line of text or a horizon across that centre and it comes back bent, with a shadow along the fold. Skies, water and blurred backgrounds cross happily — nobody can tell where a cloud was meant to be.

And the one people meet at checkout: “Design for a front cover, back cover and from 20 to 98 inside pages.” Twenty is a floor, not a suggestion.

What’s in the kit

The editable Canva template — 8 × 6 inches landscape, sized to match Canva’s own photo book product so you can build straight into it. Four guided pages: right-hand and left-hand versions of two layouts (a full-page hero and a two-photo stack), with the 0.75 in gutter keep-out already on the correct inner edge of each page and the photo frames sitting inside the half-inch safe margin. Duplicate the pages to reach your twenty — one click each.

Six reference sheets:

  • The gutter and safe-zone map — the spread drawn to scale, with the 19.05 mm keep-out either side of the fold and the safe margin, plus what may and may not cross it
  • The twenty-page planner — fill in your six sections before you place a single photo, so the minimum works for you instead of against you
  • Checking the photos — the pixels you need at every placement, and what 148 gsm gloss does to your shadows
  • The print-shop brief — a fill-in-the-blanks email that asks the layflat question for you, before you can still act on the answer
  • The binding guide — layflat against PUR, quoted from source, and the two-minute test that settles it
  • Six spread recipes — layouts that keep faces clear of the fold, drawn with the keep-out band over them

The part that costs nothing

Print any spread at home on ordinary paper, fold it down the centre, and hold it like a book. That is what the binding is going to do to it. If the fold lands on somebody’s eye, you have just saved three weeks and the price of a hardback.
