The Party Printables Kit — and the two things your printer does to your cards

Two invitations, same design, same paper, same printer. One looks like it came from a shop. The other looks like it came out of a spare room — and the difference is two settings, neither of which is in Canva.

What actually goes wrong

Your printer cannot print to the edge. Consumer machines have a non-printable margin of roughly three to six millimetres, often deeper on the trailing edge. A card designed edge to edge comes back with a white border round it.

And the print dialog shrinks your design. Every dialog — Acrobat, Chrome, Preview — defaults to some version of Fit: Fit to printable area, Shrink oversized pages, Scale to fit. It scales the document down so it sits inside the area the printer can reach. A few percent, and the card no longer fits the envelopes you already bought.

The fix costs nothing

Design inside a ten millimetre margin. Set page sizing to Actual size, never Fit. And lay four A6 cards up on one A4 sheet — they tile it exactly, because A6 is defined as a quarter of A4 — with white all round the outside of the block and the artwork touching between the cards. Cut down the shared line and the colour runs right to the edge. Full bleed, on a printer that cannot do full bleed.

What’s in the kit

The editable Canva template — four A6 pages (105 × 148 mm): the invitation, the table number, the thank-you card and a spare. Every page already has the background running full bleed and the ten millimetre type area marked, so whatever you write sits where the printer can actually reach. It is a baby shower in the example, but it is the same job for a birthday, a christening or a leaving do.

A Canva design can only hold one page size, so the A4 four-up layup and the 50 × 90 mm favour tag live on the printed sheets rather than in the template. Sheet one draws the tiling to scale — it is a two-minute job once, and then it is done for every card you ever make.

The fifteen-second check

Print one sheet and measure a card with a ruler before you cut anything. An A6 card is one hundred and five millimetres. If it measures a hundred and one, the dialog is still scaling — and no amount of redesigning will fix that.
