## The trap, and why it is deliberately not a licence clause

The previous two videos both ended on a Canva licence clause — wallpapers on the
standalone-sale restriction, real estate on model releases. A third would make
the channel sound like it has one trick.

This one is about **the twelve inches between the laptop and the printer**, and
it is two things:

1. **Home printers cannot print to the edge** — a non-printable margin of roughly
   three to six millimetres, often deeper on the trailing edge.
2. **Every print dialog defaults to some form of "Fit"** — Fit to printable area,
   Shrink oversized pages, Scale to fit — which scales the design down a few
   percent. Enough that a 5 × 7 card stops fitting 5 × 7 envelopes.

The fix is free and it is the spine of the video: a 10 mm type margin, **Actual
size / 100%** in the dialog, and four A6 cards laid up on one A4 sheet with white
round the outside and artwork touching between, so the trim cuts through the
design and gives full bleed on a printer that cannot do full bleed.

## Pin comment (after publish)

The setting is at 3:28, and it is not in Canva — it is in your print dialog.

Every dialog defaults to some version of "Fit": Fit to printable area, Shrink
oversized pages, Scale to fit. It shrinks your design so it sits inside the area
the printer can physically reach. A few percent — and the card stops fitting the
envelopes you already bought.

Set page sizing to Actual size, or 100%. Then prove it: print ONE sheet and
measure a card with a ruler. An A6 card is 105 mm wide. If it measures 101, the
dialog is still scaling and no amount of redesigning will fix it.

And the reason the four cards on the sheet touch each other is that cutting down
the shared line gives you colour right to the edge — full bleed on a printer
that cannot do full bleed.

four-up sheets and the print-dialog guide:
