# How to Make a Mockup in Canva (with a Scrolling Video for Etsy)

## Facts verified 2026-10-21

- **THE TRAP — Etsy's listing-video rules.** Etsy Help, "How to Add Listing
  Videos" (read in a browser, 21 Oct 2026): *"Your video may be 3-15 seconds
  long and will not contain any audio once uploaded to your listing."* *"The
  video aspect ratio should be 2:1 or 1:2."* *"While the minimum video
  resolution is 500px, the ideal video resolution is at least 1080px."* Maximum
  size **100 MB**; MP4, MOV and others accepted; *"You can add up to 2 videos per
  listing."* And: listing videos *"can also help improve how your listings appear
  in Etsy search."*
  The usual scrolling mockup is a 9:16 phone video, 20–30 seconds long, set to
  music. That breaks all three rules: the wrong shape, cut short, and silent.
- **CANVA'S ANIMATION CAP.** Canva Help Centre, "Apply, change, or remove
  animations": Animate → **Create an Animation** → drag the element → adjust
  the Movement Style → Done. *"Each animation can only be 10 seconds long at the
  maximum."* Animated designs download *"as videos or GIFs."* Some animations
  are Pro-tagged. Canva's motion path page: hold **Shift** while dragging to keep
  the movement straight.
- **CANVA'S MOCKUPS ARE STILLS.** Canva, "Free Online Phone Mockup Generator":
  Elements → **Mockups** → **Smartphones**, drag your design onto the mockup,
  **Edit** to change fit, alignment, size or orientation, **Apply changes**, then
  download *"as a high-quality PDF, SVG, JPG, or PNG"*. No video option — so the
  scrolling version has to be built separately.
- **THE TALL IMAGE.** Canva's maximum design size is 8000 × 3125 px per axis
  (verified 6 Oct 2026 for the infographics video) — a 1080 × 2160 video page
  fits, and a stacked screenshot can be up to 3,125 tall.

NOT CLAIMED: what Etsy does to a video that is not 1:2 or 2:1, how much a video
lifts sales, Etsy's fees, or which mockups are free — the script tells viewers
to check the label on each one.

## S01 — HOOK (~120 words)

> The scrolling phone mockup most Etsy sellers make breaks Etsy's own rules
> three ways at once.
>
> Etsy's help page is specific. A listing video can be three to fifteen seconds
> long. It plays with no sound — the audio is removed when you upload it. And
> the shape should be two to one, or one to two.
>
> The usual scrolling mockup is a tall phone video, thirty seconds long, set to
> music. Wrong shape, cut short, and the music you spent ten minutes choosing is
> stripped out before a single shopper sees it.
>
> And Canva's mockups are stills — they download as pictures, not video.
>
> There is a free Etsy listing video card in the description. Let me build both
> properly — the photo and the video.

## S02 — THE FREE CARD, AND THREE PRINCIPLES (~115 words)

> The free card is linked below — Etsy's video rules quoted, Canva's ten-second
> limit, and the size to build at, on one page.
>
> Three principles.
>
> One: the mockup photo sells the look, and the video shows what is inside. They
> are two different jobs, so they are two different files.
>
> Two: design for silence. Etsy removes the audio, so anything you want a shopper
> to know has to be written on the screen.
>
> Three: build to Etsy's shape and length from the start, rather than finding out
> afterwards what it does to yours.
>
> Photo for the look, video for the inside, silent first.

## S03 — THE MOCKUP PHOTO (~155 words)

> Start with the photo, because this is the part Canva makes easy.
>
> Open any design, go to Elements, and choose Mockups. Under Smartphones you get
> a library of phones — flat on a desk, in a hand, on a sofa, straight on.
>
> Pick one, then drag your design straight onto the phone's screen. Canva fits it
> to the screen and bends it to the angle of the phone, so a straight design
> looks like it is really on that device.
>
> If it sits wrong, select Edit on the mockup. That is where the fit, the
> alignment, the size and the orientation live. Adjust it, then Apply changes.
>
> Then download. And notice the options: P D F, S V G, J P G or P N G. Every one
> of them is a picture.
>
> That is your listing photo. It is excellent at what it does. It just does not
> move — and the thing shoppers most want to see is what happens when they scroll.

## S04 — CHOOSING THE MOCKUP (~150 words)

> Before you go on, a word about which phone to pick, because it matters more
> than it looks.
>
> Pick the setting your buyer lives in. A planner sells on a desk beside a coffee.
> A wallpaper sells in a hand. A social media template sells on a phone held up,
> slightly angled, like someone is about to post.
>
> Straight-on mockups are the most honest — the design is not distorted, so
> nobody is disappointed when they open the file. Angled ones look better in a
> thumbnail. Use one of each.
>
> And check every mockup's label before you rely on it. Some are part of the free
> library and some are not, and the label is the only thing that tells you.
>
> Make three or four photos: the cover on a phone, one inner page, one in a
> real-life setting, and one flat-lay. The video comes next, and it does the job
> none of those can.

## S05 — THE TALL SCREENSHOT (~150 words)

> The scroll needs something to scroll through, so build that first.
>
> Make a new design one thousand and eighty pixels wide and as tall as your
> product needs — up to three thousand one hundred and twenty five, which is
> Canva's height limit.
>
> Stack your pages in it, top to bottom, the way a buyer would actually move
> through them. The cover first, then the pages that show the most variety, and
> the best page last, because the last thing on screen is what they remember.
>
> Leave a small gap between pages, the same gap every time, so the scroll reads as
> separate pages rather than one long smear.
>
> Download it as a P N G — a sharp still image of everything at once.
>
> That single tall picture is what will move. Everything about the video is just
> deciding how it moves, and how long for.

## S06 — THE TRAP, DEMONSTRATED (~190 words)

> Here is the version most people make, so you can see exactly where it goes
> wrong.
>
> A phone-shaped video — nine by sixteen, one thousand and eighty by one thousand
> nine hundred and twenty. The tall screenshot scrolling slowly for thirty
> seconds. A nice music track underneath.
>
> Now hold it against Etsy's page. Three to fifteen seconds — thirty is twice the
> limit. No audio once uploaded — the music is gone. And the shape should be two
> to one or one to two. Nine by sixteen is neither.
>
> Here is the same thing, rebuilt to the rules. A custom size of one thousand and
> eighty by two thousand one hundred and sixty. That is exactly one to two, and it
> is the shape of a phone screen anyway.
>
> The scroll is ten seconds — and that is not a choice. Canva's help page says
> each animation can be ten seconds long at the maximum. Two seconds of the cover
> before it starts, two seconds of the last page after it stops. Fourteen seconds.
>
> And no music at all. The words on screen do the talking, because on Etsy they
> are the only thing that will.

## S07 — MAKING THE SCROLL (~160 words)

> So, the scroll itself, step by step.
>
> New design, custom size, one thousand and eighty by two thousand one hundred and
> sixty. Drop your tall P N G onto the page and stretch it to the full width, so
> the top of the screenshot lines up with the top of the page.
>
> Select it. On the toolbar, Animate, then Create an Animation. Now drag the image
> upwards — and hold Shift while you drag, which Canva says keeps the movement
> straight. Drag until the bottom of the screenshot meets the bottom of the page.
>
> Choose the movement style, and set the speed so the move fills the ten seconds.
> Then Done.
>
> Then build the holds as pages, not as tricks. Page one: the screenshot sitting
> still at the top, two seconds. Page two: the scroll, ten seconds. Page three:
> the screenshot sitting still at the bottom, two seconds. Set each page's length
> on the timeline, and leave the transitions off so it reads as one shot.
>
> Play it back once at full screen. If any page flies past too fast to read, the
> screenshot is too tall for ten seconds. Take a page out rather than speeding it
> up.

## S08 — PUTTING A PHONE AROUND IT (~150 words)

> You can stop there — on Etsy, a full-screen scroll at one to two already looks
> like a phone. But if you want the phone itself in shot, here is the honest way.
>
> Don't try to animate inside a Canva mockup — the mockups are stills, and the
> motion path moves whatever you drag as a whole. Build a window instead.
>
> Put a phone shape on top of your scrolling screenshot: a rounded rectangle for
> the body, with the screen area left empty. Then cover everything outside the
> screen with four plain rectangles in your background colour — one above, one
> below, one each side.
>
> Now the screenshot scrolls behind them, and you only ever see it through the
> screen-shaped gap. That is all a scrolling mockup is.
>
> Keep the phone straight on. A tilted phone with a straight scroll inside it
> looks wrong immediately, and you cannot fake the angle in a ten-second move.

## S09 — DESIGN FOR SILENCE (~150 words)

> Then the part almost every scrolling video gets wrong: it was made with the
> sound on.
>
> Etsy removes the audio. So go through your video with the sound off and ask
> what a shopper actually learns.
>
> Put the important facts on the screen as text. How many pages. What size. That
> it is editable in Canva. One short line at a time, big enough to read on a phone
> held at arm's length.
>
> Put the first line on the very first frame. Shoppers are scrolling past dozens
> of listings, and whatever is on screen in the first second is all most of them
> will ever see.
>
> And don't write anything that only makes sense with a voice — no "watch this",
> no "here's the best bit". On Etsy there is nobody saying it.
>
> A silent video that explains itself beats a beautiful one that needs a
> soundtrack.

## S10 — THE EXPORT (~140 words)

> The export, and three numbers to check before it goes anywhere.
>
> Download, and choose M P 4 video — Canva's help says animated designs download
> as videos or GIFs, and Etsy wants a video.
>
> The length: under fifteen seconds. Look at the duration before you upload, not
> after.
>
> The size: Etsy's limit is one hundred megabytes. A fifteen-second clip is
> nowhere near that, so if yours is, something has gone wrong with the design —
> usually a page that is far bigger than it needs to be.
>
> The shape: one to two, which is what you built. Ten eighty by twenty one sixty.
> Etsy says the ideal resolution is at least ten eighty, so you are already there.
>
> If any of the three is wrong, fix it in Canva. Never re-cut the file somewhere
> else and hope.

## S11 — TWO VIDEOS, NOT ONE (~140 words)

> One more thing from Etsy's own page that most sellers never use.
>
> You can add two videos per listing. Almost everyone adds one, or none.
>
> So make the second one do a different job. The first is the scroll — what is
> inside. The second can be what happens after they buy: the template opening in
> Canva, a name being typed in, a colour being changed. Proof that it is editable,
> which is the question every digital buyer is really asking.
>
> Build it the same way. One to two, under fifteen seconds, silent, text on screen.
>
> Etsy's help page also says listing videos can help improve how listings appear
> in Etsy search. That is their claim, not mine, and it is the best reason there is
> to spend ten more minutes on a second one.

## S12 — MAKE IT A TEMPLATE (~120 words)

> One habit, and it pays back on every listing you ever make.
>
> Once the scroll works, keep the design. Next product, swap the tall screenshot,
> and the size, the timing, the holds and the text styles are already right.
>
> Do the same with the window, if you built one. The phone body and the four
> rectangles never change — only the picture behind them does.
>
> And keep a note: the page size, the length of the scroll, and how tall the
> screenshot was. The thing that goes wrong on listing twenty is a screenshot twice
> as tall as the first one, scrolling twice as fast in the same ten seconds.
>
> One template, every listing.

## S13 — THE CHECKS (~110 words)

> Before it goes on Etsy.
>
> The page is one thousand and eighty by two thousand one hundred and sixty — one
> to two. The video is under fifteen seconds. The scroll is ten seconds or less.
> Every page is readable as it passes. The important facts are on screen as text,
> and it makes sense with the sound off. The first frame says what the product is.
> And you have made the second video, not just the first.
>
> Seven things. The fifth is the one nobody checks — because they never watch
> their own video silent.
