**Facts verified 5 Aug 2026:**
- Canva JPG/PNG downloads are **96 DPI**; **PDF Print** is the only native
  300-DPI export. Canva Pro's 2×/3× size multiplier raises pixel count.
- Buyer-standard ratios: **2:3, 3:4, 4:5, ISO A-series** (+ 11×14 which fits
  none of them — needs its own file).
- Canva licence: you may sell an **original design** using Free/Pro content;
  you may **not** sell Canva stock content standalone (a stock photo or a
  single Pro element printed as-is).
- Etsy listings should state file types, sizes, resolution and usage terms.

## S03 — THE RATIO PROBLEM (~155 words)

> Now the part that decides whether buyers can actually use your file. Frame
> sizes are not one shape. In the US, buyers own four-by-six, five-by-seven,
> eight-by-ten, eleven-by-fourteen, sixteen-by-twenty, twenty-four-by-thirty
> — and in Europe, A5 down to A1. Here's the good news: most of those
> collapse into three ratios. Two-to-three covers four-by-six, eight-by-
> twelve, sixteen-by-twenty-four. Three-to-four covers six-by-eight,
> nine-by-twelve, eighteen-by-twenty-four. Four-to-five covers eight-by-ten
> and sixteen-by-twenty — and four-to-five is the single most popular
> ratio in printable art. Add the ISO A-series for European buyers, and one
> awkward outlier: eleven-by-fourteen fits none of the above, so it needs
> its own file. That's five files per design, and buyers expect all five.

## S04 — CANVAS SETUP, THE RIGHT WAY (~150 words)

> So how do you build five sizes without designing five times? Set your
> canvas in inches at the largest size of the ratio, not the smallest —
> scaling down stays sharp, scaling up never does. For four-to-five, that's
> a sixteen-by-twenty-inch canvas. Create a design, Custom size, switch
> units to inches, sixteen by twenty. Design once at that size. Then use
> Resize — Canva Pro's Resize and Magic Switch — to spit out the other
> ratios, and nudge your elements to fit each new shape; it is not fully
> automatic, and anyone who tells you it is has never sold a print. If
> you're on the free plan, duplicate the design and set each canvas
> manually. Design big, resize down, always check each one by eye.

[VISUAL: custom size types 16 × 20 in; Resize panel opens; 3 ratios spawn;
nudge-to-fit beat; free-plan fallback card.]

## S05 — DESIGN THE PIECE (~175 words)

> Let's actually make one. I'll build a typography piece because it's the
> fastest thing to sell that's unambiguously yours. Background first: not
> pure white — a warm off-white, something like F5F1EA, prints softer and
> looks more expensive on a wall. Add your text, and here's where taste
> lives: one statement word or short phrase, one font, enormous. Then let
> it breathe — printable art needs generous margins, at least ten percent
> of the canvas on every side, because buyers will frame it and the mat
> board eats the edges. Add one supporting element — a thin rule, a small
> abstract shape, an arch — and stop. The most common beginner mistake is
> five ideas fighting on one canvas. Then the set: duplicate the page twice
> and change only the word and the accent, so the three pieces read as a
> family. Same font, same margins, same palette. That's a gallery wall.

## S06 — THE 300 DPI TRAP (~180 words)

> Now the number that causes the refunds. When you download a PNG or JPG
> from Canva, it comes out at ninety-six DPI — screen resolution, not print
> resolution. Print wants three hundred. A ninety-six DPI file will look
> perfect on your monitor and print soft and pixelated at sixteen by
> twenty, and your buyer will leave a two-star review saying "blurry". Two
> ways to fix it, and only one is free. The free way: download as PDF
> Print. Canva's PDF Print export is genuinely three hundred DPI — it's the
> only native export that is — and print shops accept PDFs happily. The
> Pro way: when you download a PNG, open Size and set the multiplier to
> two-x or three-x. Because you designed at sixteen by twenty inches, three-x
> gives you a file with enough pixels for a true three-hundred-DPI print.
> Ship both: the PDF for print shops, the high-multiplier PNG or JPG for
> home printers and photo labs. Never ship a default one-x PNG. That single
> setting is the difference between a five-star shop and a refund queue.

[VISUAL: download menu — PNG at 1x with "96 DPI" flag ✕; PDF Print with
"300 DPI ✓"; Pro Size multiplier slider 1x→3x with pixel counter climbing;
side-by-side sharp vs pixelated crop.]

## S07 — THE LICENCE RULES (~150 words)

> Before you list anything — the rules, because getting this wrong can cost
> you your shop. Canva's licence lets you sell an original design that uses
> Canva content, free or Pro. What it does not let you do is sell Canva's
> content standalone: you cannot take a stock photo, or one Pro
> illustration, print it as-is, and sell that. The test is simple — if you
> removed Canva's asset, would there still be a design? If the answer is
> no, it isn't yours to sell. Typography pieces, abstract compositions, and
> layouts you arranged pass easily. A single stock photograph with nothing
> added does not. And you cannot claim a trademark on anything containing
> Canva content. Design originally and this is a non-issue — which is
> exactly why typography and shape-based art is the smart place to start.

[VISUAL: two cards — "original design ✓" (type + shapes composed) vs "stock

## S09 — PACKAGING THE DOWNLOAD (~155 words)

> Now package it so it arrives like a professional product. Etsy gives you
> five file slots, twenty megabytes each. Name your files so they're
> obvious in a downloads folder: not "final-3.pdf" but
> "Neutral-Arch-Set-4x5-16x20in-300dpi.pdf". Include one PDF per ratio,
> plus the eleven-by-fourteen, and a short read-me PDF: what's included,
> the recommended paper — matte or lightly textured, two hundred GSM or
> heavier — where to print, and a line telling them not to scale the file
> up when printing, because printer software will happily offer to. If
> your set is bigger than five files, zip it — but only if you have to,
> because every zip generates at least one "how do I open this" message.
> Personal use only, stated plainly, in the file and the listing.
