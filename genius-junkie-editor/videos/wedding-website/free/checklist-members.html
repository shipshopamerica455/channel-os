The trap — the RSVP form belongs to whoever made it

Every reply lands in the form owner's Responses tab — not the couple's. From Google Docs Editors Help, "View & manage form responses": "form owners can download and delete existing responses."

So if you build a wedding site for someone else, or sell it as a template, with your Google Form behind the RSVP button, every guest list ends up in your account — and the couple cannot see a single reply. Sharing the form does not fix it; that gives them access to yours. They need their own copy.

  • Your own wedding — nothing to do. You made the form, so the replies are yours.
  • Built for someone else — they make the form in their own Google account, or open yours and use More → Make a copy (Google's own help).
  • Selling the template — a Google Docs Editors community guide describes changing the end of the form's address to /copy, which asks whoever opens it to make their own copy. That is a community guide, not an official policy page — test it before you rely on it.

Then let Google close it for you: in the form, under Accepting responses, choose Set close date. Late RSVPs after the caterer has the numbers are worse than none.

Canva's publishing rules — the template decides the address

From the Canva Help Centre, "Publish websites to a free domain":

  • A free .canva.site address needs a design made from a Website template.
  • Mobile website and Link in Bio templates publish to a canva.link address only, with no custom domain.
  • "Canva Free users can have up to five live websites on the free domain."
  • The Designed with Canva footer stays on Free plans; removing it needs Pro.
  • Steps: Publish website → Site address → Settings → Continue → Publish.

The address is a promise. Once it is printed on an invitation it cannot change — choose the name, publish, and only then print it.

The phone — most guests never see the desktop version

  • Turn on Resize on mobile in the publishing settings — then do not trust it.
  • Preview in mobile view and go through every page, top to bottom.
  • Look for text that moved, text shrunk to nothing, and buttons too small for a thumb. Anything side by side on desktop has to stack on a phone, and the stacking is where it breaks.
  • The countdown is made with Canva Code — Canva's own wedding page says so. There is no countdown in Elements. Check the time zone.

Getting it to the guests

  • Test the link from a phone that is not yours — logged in to Canva, you may not see what a guest sees.
  • For a QR code on the printed invitation: large, on a plain background, and scanned from the printed proof. A code that works on a laptop and fails on paper is the most common way this goes wrong.

Seven checks before you send the address to anyone

  • The design was made from a Website template
  • The RSVP button points at a form the couple owns — not you
  • You filled in the RSVP yourself on the published site, and it arrived in the right account
  • Resize on mobile is on, and every page was checked in mobile view
  • The RSVP button is big enough to tap with a thumb
  • The form has a close date
  • The QR code scans from the printed proof, not just the screen

The second is the one that goes wrong silently — and nobody finds out until the guest list is missing.
