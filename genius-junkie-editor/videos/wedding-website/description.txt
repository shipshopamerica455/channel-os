The RSVP form on your Canva wedding website probably belongs to you, not the
couple — and every guest who replies is filling in your form.

Google's own help page is plain about it: responses are collected in the form,
and "form owners can download and delete existing responses". So if you built
the site for a friend, or you sell it as a template, every guest list ends up in
your account and the couple cannot see a single reply. Sharing the form does not
fix it — that only gives them access to yours. They need their own copy.

This builds the whole site properly: the template that decides your address,
four pages, the countdown (it is not in Elements), an RSVP that reaches the right
person, a site that survives a phone, and getting the address onto the printed
invitation.

THE FIX, THREE WAYS
· Your own wedding — nothing to do. You made the form; the replies are yours.
· Built for someone else — they make the form in their own Google account, or
  open yours and use More → Make a copy (Google's own help pages).
· Selling the template — a Google Docs Editors COMMUNITY guide describes changing
  the end of the form's address to /copy, which asks whoever opens it to make
  their own copy. That is a community guide, not an official policy page — test
  it before you rely on it.
Then let Google close it on the deadline: Accepting responses → Set close date.

CANVA'S PUBLISHING RULES (Canva Help Centre, "Publish websites to a free domain")
· A free .canva.site address needs a design made from a WEBSITE template.
· Mobile website and Link in Bio templates publish to canva.link only, and do not
  support a custom domain.
· "Canva Free users can have up to five live websites on the free domain."
· The Designed with Canva footer stays on Free plans.
· "Turn on Resize on mobile to optimise your site for mobile devices." — then
  check every page in mobile view anyway.
· The countdown: Canva's own wedding page says interactive pieces like "a
  countdown timer" are made "using Canva Code".
