⚠️ The part that decides whether it works

  1. Share → Download → PDF Standard, not PDF Print. Print is for paper and gives you a file several times the size for a difference nobody can see on a screen.
  2. Never attach the PDF to the delivery email. Attachments are one of the most reliable ways to land in spam — Mailchimp's own documentation says they can slow or stop bulk delivery, or get the message blocked outright, which is why it hosts your file for you instead (capped at 10 MB).
  3. Upload the PDF somewhere it lives — your email tool's own file hosting, your website, or a cloud drive with the link set to anyone-with-the-link — and put a button in the email that points at it.
  4. Then sign up to your own form, with a real address, on a phone, and go all the way through. Did it arrive? Did it land in promotions? Did the button work?

Where the addresses go

Canva can capture an email now: drop a form into a design, share it by public link, and the addresses land in a Canva Sheet. Free, five minutes, and genuinely fine for your first fifty. Just be clear about what you've got — a Sheet is a list of addresses, not an email list. It doesn't send the file and it doesn't follow anybody up.
