The Real Estate Listing Kit — and the clause most agents have never read

Estate agents reach for the same stock photo every week: a smiling couple holding a set of keys. It comes free with the design software, it looks professional, and it goes straight onto the listing flyer. There is a sentence in Canva’s own licence agreement about that photograph that changes what you can do with it.

Canva’s Content License Agreement, verbatim

“Releases are generally not obtained for Free Content, and some jurisdictions provide legal protection against a person’s image, likeness or property being used for commercial purposes when they have not provided a release.”

Read the middle of that: image, likeness or property. Canva names property explicitly. Property marketing is unambiguously commercial use, and the same agreement puts the job of deciding whether a release is needed — and obtaining it — on you.

So the rule is simpler than the paragraph that produced it: no stock people anywhere in property marketing. Not on the flyer, not on the window card, and never on a testimonial card, because a stock headshot beside “sold in two weeks, wonderful service” presents a model as a genuine client endorsing your business.

This is not legal advice, and agents are regulated differently in every market. What the kit gives you is the sentence to take to your broker.

The part that costs nothing

Your window card gets about three seconds from somebody walking past. That buys three pieces of information: the price, the location, and one photograph. Put the price in the largest type on the page, give the photograph the top half, and put everything else in a band at the bottom in type you would call too small. It is not too small.
