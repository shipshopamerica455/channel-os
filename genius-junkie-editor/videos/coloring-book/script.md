# How to Make a Coloring Book in Canva for Amazon KDP

## Facts verified 2026-09-24 — all from Amazon KDP's own help pages

- **THE TRAP — the minimum line weight.** Paperback Submission Guidelines, word
  for word: *"If your book contains lines (i.e., a chart, table or graphic
  element), give the lines a minimum thickness/weight of 0.75 point or 0.01"
  (0.3 mm)."* A coloring book is nothing but lines. Canva's default stroke
  weights and traced AI line art routinely sit under that floor, and thin lines
  print broken or disappear.
  Source: Paperback Submission Guidelines.
- **Bleed is NOT symmetrical.** *"extend them 0.125" (3.2 mm) beyond the final
  trim size from the top, bottom, and outer edges"* and *"Format your PDF
  manuscript at 0.25" (6.4 mm) higher and 0.125" (3.2 mm) wider than your
  selected trim size."* Height gains an eighth twice; width gains it once,
  because the spine edge gets none. Canva's bleed is uniform on all four sides.
  Source: Set Trim Size, Bleed, and Margins.
- **Inside margin by page count.** 24–150 pages = 0.375"; 151–300 = 0.5";
  301–500 = 0.625"; 501–700 = 0.75"; 701–828 = 0.875". Outside minimum is 0.25"
  without bleed and 0.375" with bleed. Same source.
- **Minimum page count is 24**, and *"We'll calculate your page count based on
  your manuscript file, rounding up to an even number if necessary."*
- **Images at least 300 DPI**, *"All fonts in the interior files should be
  embedded"*, and *"Flatten transparent objects and layers in the native file
  before publishing."*
- **Custom trim range:** *"Width must be within 4"-8.5"... Height must be within
  6"-11.69"."*
- **Canva PDF Standard is 96 DPI; PDF Print is 300 DPI** — verified for the
  pitch-deck video, Canva Help Centre, "Download designs as flattened PDF".

NOT CLAIMED, because Amazon does not document it: anything about single-sided
printing, royalties, or printing cost. Blank backing pages are described as a
convention buyers expect, never as an Amazon rule.

## S01 — HOOK (~110 words)

> Amazon has a published minimum line thickness, and almost every coloring book
> made in Canva breaks it. Their words: give the lines a minimum thickness of
> three quarters of a point. A coloring book is nothing but lines. Canva's
> default stroke is thinner than that, and so is most traced art, so the file
> uploads, it passes review, it goes on sale — and the printed copy comes back
> with the fine lines broken up or gone. Nobody rejects it. The customer just
> leaves a one star review. There is a free spec card in the description with
> every number Amazon actually requires. And now I will show you how to build
> the book so this never happens.

## S02 — THE FREE CARD, AND THREE PRINCIPLES (~110 words)

> The free K D P coloring book spec card is linked below — one page, every
> number, printed and stuck to the wall.
>
> Three principles before we open Canva. One: decide bleed before you set the
> page size, because bleed changes the size of the document and you cannot
> change it later without redrawing everything. Two: the inside margin is not a
> style choice, it is a number that depends on how many pages the book has.
> Three: every line you draw has a floor, and scaling art down pushes it under
> that floor without telling you. Get those three right and the rest is drawing.

## S03 — TRIM SIZE, AND WHY IT COMES FIRST (~140 words)

> Open Canva and choose Custom size. The standard coloring book is eight and a
> half by eleven inches, and I would start there — it is the size buyers expect
> and the size most listings use. Amazon lets you go custom, but inside limits:
> width between four and eight and a half inches, height between six and eleven
> point six nine inches. Outside that, the book will not print.
>
> Set the unit to inches before you type anything. Canva defaults to pixels, and
> eight and a half pixels is a postage stamp.
>
> Now stop, because the next decision changes this number. Do your designs touch
> the edge of the page? A border that runs off the paper, a background pattern,
> a full page scene? If yes, you need bleed, and bleed makes the document bigger.

## S04 — THE BLEED MATHS, WHICH IS NOT SYMMETRICAL (~140 words)

> Here is where most people get it wrong, and it is Amazon's own arithmetic.
>
> Bleed is an eighth of an inch, and it goes on the top, the bottom, and the
> outer edge. Not the inner edge. The inner edge is the spine — it is glued, so
> nothing bleeds there.
>
> So the height gains an eighth twice, top and bottom, which is a quarter of an
> inch. The width gains an eighth only once. Amazon writes it out: format the
> P D F a quarter inch higher and an eighth of an inch wider than your trim size.
>
> On an eight and a half by eleven book that is a page eight point six two five
> wide by eleven point two five high.
>
> Canva does not know about spines. Its bleed sits on all four sides evenly. So
> do not use Canva's bleed toggle for this — type the bled size in as your custom
> size and keep control of it yourself.

## S05 — MARGINS, AND THE NUMBER THAT MOVES (~160 words)

> Now the margins, and this is the one that catches people on their second book.
>
> The inside margin — the gutter, against the spine — depends on the page count.
> Twenty four to a hundred and fifty pages, three eighths of an inch. A hundred
> and fifty one to three hundred, half an inch. Three hundred and one to five
> hundred, five eighths. It keeps growing, because a thicker book curves harder
> into the spine and swallows more of the page.
>
> The outside margin has a floor too: a quarter of an inch if you are not using
> bleed, three eighths if you are.
>
> So set your guides now. File, Settings, Add guides, custom — and place them at
> those numbers. Everything you draw lives inside the guides.
>
> And note what Amazon says: if your page count changes, go back and check the
> inside margin, because it may need to change too. Add twenty pages late and
> your gutter can quietly become wrong on every single page.

## S06 — THE TRAP: THREE QUARTERS OF A POINT (~180 words)

> Now the thing nobody tells you.
>
> Amazon publishes a minimum line thickness. Their words: if your book contains
> lines — a chart, a table, a graphic element — give the lines a minimum
> thickness of three quarters of a point, or one hundredth of an inch, or three
> tenths of a millimetre.
>
> A coloring book is lines. That is the entire product. And three quarters of a
> point is thicker than you think.
>
> Watch. I draw a line in Canva with the pen tool. Default weight. I open the
> stroke panel — it is one point, so that one is fine. Now here is a shape from
> the elements panel, a line drawing of a flower. I select it, and the stroke is
> nought point five. Under the floor. It looks perfect on my screen at four
> hundred percent zoom, and it will print as a broken dotted line, or not at all.
>
> Nothing warns you. The file uploads. The preview looks right, because the
> preview is a screen and a screen has no ink.

## S07 — THE FIX, AND THE SCALING TRAP UNDER IT (~150 words)

> The fix is one habit: check every stroke before it goes on the page, and set
> the floor at one point, not three quarters. Give yourself the margin.
>
> But here is the part that catches careful people. Scaling.
>
> If you take a drawing with a one point stroke and shrink it to half size,
> Canva scales the stroke with it. Your one point line is now half a point. You
> did not change the stroke — you changed the size, and the stroke came along.
>
> So the rule is: scale first, check the stroke second. Never the other way
> round. Resize the art to exactly the size it will print, then select it and
> read the stroke weight, then fix it. Every element, every page.
>
> This is why a book can look flawless in Canva and print like a photocopy of a
> photocopy.

## S08 — PAGE COUNT, AND THE EVEN NUMBER (~150 words)

> Page count. The minimum is twenty four pages, and Amazon rounds up to an even
> number if it needs to. So a twenty three page book becomes twenty four, and a
> forty one page book becomes forty two — and that extra page is blank and it is
> at the back, where you did not plan for it.
>
> Decide the count deliberately instead.
>
> And here is the convention buyers expect, which is a design decision rather
> than an Amazon rule: a coloring page with another coloring page printed on its
> back is a problem, because markers and gel pens soak through. So most coloring
> books put the artwork on one side and leave the other side blank. That doubles
> your page count. Forty drawings becomes eighty pages. Plan the number from the
> start, because remember — change the page count and you have to go back and
> check the gutter.

## S09 — BUILDING A PAGE, START TO FINISH (~150 words)

> Let us build one properly. New page. The guides are already there.
>
> I am dropping in line art — this one is a wreath. First thing, not the last
> thing: I scale it to the size it will print. Now I select it and open the
> stroke panel. Nought point seven five. Technically legal, but it is sitting
> exactly on the floor, so I push it to one point. Better.
>
> Next, it has to sit inside the guides. The outer edges are easy. The inner
> edge is the one people miss — that is the gutter, three eighths of an inch on
> a book this size, and anything that crosses it disappears into the spine when
> the book is glued.
>
> And notice I have left the facing page empty. That is deliberate. That is the
> blank back we just talked about.

## S10 — EXPORTING, WHERE THE RESOLUTION DIES (~150 words)

> Now the export, and this is the second place Canva will quietly ruin the file.
>
> Share, Download, and the file type menu. P D F Standard is the default, and
> P D F Standard is ninety six D P I. Amazon requires images at a minimum of
> three hundred D P I. Ninety six is a third of what they ask for.
>
> Choose P D F Print instead. That is three hundred D P I.
>
> Underneath it you get two checkboxes. Crop marks and bleed — leave that
> unticked, because you already built the bleed into your page size, and ticking
> it adds a second lot on top. Flatten P D F — tick that one. Amazon asks you to
> flatten transparent objects and layers before publishing, and that checkbox is
> exactly that. It also embeds your fonts, which Amazon also requires.
>
> Two boxes. One on, one off.

## S11 — WHAT TO CHECK BEFORE YOU UPLOAD (~140 words)

> Before it goes anywhere, open the P D F you just exported — not the Canva
> design, the actual file — and zoom to one hundred percent. Not four hundred.
> One hundred percent is roughly print size, and at print size a line that is
> too thin starts to look grey and uncertain instead of black and solid.
>
> Check the page dimensions in your P D F reader. They should be the bled
> numbers you typed, to the decimal. If the width gained a quarter inch instead
> of an eighth, you ticked the crop marks box.
>
> Count your pages, and make sure it is even.
>
> Then upload it to K D P and open their previewer. It shows you the trim line
> and the gutter, and it is free to look at as many times as you like before you
> publish.

## S12 — THE COVER IS A SEPARATE FILE (~120 words)

> One thing that trips people at the last step: the interior and the cover are
> two separate uploads. What we have just built is the interior only.
>
> The cover is one flat piece of artwork — back, spine and front in a single
> image — and its size depends on your page count, because the spine gets wider
> as the book gets thicker. Amazon has a cover template generator that gives you
> the exact dimensions once you tell it your page count and paper type.
>
> Get the interior finished and the page count locked first. Then generate the
> cover template. Doing it the other way round means making the cover twice.

## S13 — THE FOUR NUMBERS (~100 words)

> So. Four numbers, and the book is right.
>
> Three quarters of a point is the minimum line weight, and I would work at one
> point to be safe. An eighth of an inch is bleed, twice on the height, once on
> the width, never on the spine. Three eighths of an inch is your inside margin
> at this page count. And three hundred D P I means P D F Print, not P D F
> Standard.
>
> Scale first, check the stroke second. That is the habit that separates a book
> that prints from a book that prints badly.
