The Lookbook Kit — print-ready A4, and the colour setting nobody mentions

A lookbook is the one Canva design people actually send to a printer. It is also where two settings quietly ruin the job — and one of them is behind the paywall in a way almost nobody writes about.

Canva's own help page, twice

PDF Print is described as offering a "color profile (RGB or CMYK) selection option". The same page lists CMYK colour profile options among the features restricted to Pro, Teams, Education and Nonprofit accounts.

So PDF Print works on a free plan. The CMYK choice inside it does not — which means your print file leaves in RGB and the print shop's software decides what your brand colours become. Neon orange, electric blue and lime collapse. Deep teal, burgundy and charcoal barely move. Nobody tells you it happened; you just get a box of catalogues that look flat.

What's in the kit

The editable Canva template — A4 landscape, 297 × 210 mm, with the 5 mm margins and the grid already in place. Cover, six spreads sharing one layout, and a contact page that actually says how to buy.

  • Bleed and trim map — the page, the 3 mm bleed, the trim line and the safe margin, drawn to scale
  • Photo prep — the 100%-zoom check, and the minimum pixels for every placement at 300 dpi

  • Export checklist — the print version against the screen version, side by side
  • Six layout recipes — the spreads from the template, as diagrams

The half that costs nothing

The second trap is free to fix. A guillotine is accurate to about a millimetre, and a millimetre of drift on a coloured page leaves a white stripe down the edge. Run the background past the canvas and tick Crop marks and bleed at download. The kit shows exactly how far past, and why.
