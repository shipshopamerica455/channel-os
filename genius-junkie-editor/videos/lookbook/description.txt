A lookbook is the one Canva design people actually send to a printer — and it is where two settings quietly ruin the job. This is the full build, plus the part nobody mentions: the colour profile you need for print is behind Canva's paywall.

Canva's own help page says PDF Print offers a "color profile (RGB or CMYK) selection option" — and the same page lists CMYK colour profile options among the features restricted to Pro, Teams, Education and Nonprofit accounts. So PDF Print works on a free plan. The CMYK choice inside it does not, which means your print file leaves in RGB and somebody else's software decides what your brand colours become. Bright orange, electric blue and anything neon land noticeably duller. Deep teal, burgundy and charcoal barely move.

There are three honest ways round it on a free account, and all three are in the video.

The second trap is free to fix: bleed. A guillotine is accurate to about a millimetre, and a millimetre of drift on a coloured page leaves a white stripe down the edge. Run the background past the trim and tick "Crop marks and bleed" at download.
