# How to Make Printable Christmas Gift Tags in Canva (10 Per Sheet)

## Facts verified 2026-11-02 — Canva's own Help Centre, read today

- **THE TRAP — one row, one page.** Canva Help, "Create designs in bulk" (read 2 Nov
  2026): *"Each row creates one version of your design."* Output **"1 design with
  multiple pages: Creates one design with a separate page for each row of data."**
  (or "Individual designs" — a separate design per row). The page does NOT describe
  putting several rows on one page. So thirty names in one column = thirty pages,
  and printed, each page is a sheet of paper. **THE FIX (also from that page):**
  *"For text, add a separate text box for each field."* *"You can connect up to 150
  data fields."* → ten text boxes on one sheet, ten columns (Name 1 … Name 10), one
  row per SHEET. Thirty names = three rows = three sheets.
- **Where it is / what the buttons say (UI as of today — changed since the Aug
  certificates video):** click the page background → **Edit** → **Bulk create**, or
  Apps → More from Canva → Bulk create. After adding data: **"Match and fill
  fields"** (auto-match); change a match: select element → **Match data**.
  **(•••) More options** next to **Create designs** → Output. Final button
  **"Create [number] designs"**. Data: Canva Sheet, CSV or XLSX, connected app, or a
  typed table. Canva Sheets: up to 300 rows, 150 columns.
- **PLANS:** *"Bulk Create is available on desktop web and the Canva desktop app for
  Canva Pro, Canva Teams, Canva Business"*, plus Enterprise, Education and Nonprofit.
  **Not Canva Free.** (Help page, read today.) S08 is the free route.
- **CROP MARKS:** Canva Help, "Margins, bleed and crop marks" (read today): *"Crop
  marks only appear in exported PDFs and not inside the Canva editor."* Download →
  PDF **Print** → Advanced settings → "Crop marks and bleed", **off by default**.
  *"Note that the size for crop marks is fixed."* Bleed is 0.125 in / 3.175 mm,
  *"cannot be adjusted"*. Crop marks mark the trim of the PAGE — the page says
  nothing about marks for items inside a page. Script line is therefore: crop marks
  mark the page edge, and on this layout the page edge is already a cut edge.
- **GUIDES:** Canva Help, "Adding and removing guides" (read today): File →
  Rulers and guides → **Add guides** → **Custom** (or presets 12 columns, 3 columns,
  3×3 Grid) → Add guides. Guides are locked by default. *"Rulers show pixel
  dimensions… not the final physical product."* Guides do not print — so the cut
  lines are drawn as real lines.
- **PRINTER MARGINS** (carried from the baby-shower video's verified block): home
  inkjets and lasers cannot print to the paper edge — commonly 3–6 mm per side.

OUR LAYOUT (our design choice, stated as ours): page = US Letter 8.5 × 11 in or
A4. Custom guides: 2 columns × 5 rows, no margin, so ten tags fill the page and
the page edge is the outer edge of the outer tags. Tag = 4.25 × 2.2 in on Letter,
105 × 59.4 mm on A4. Everything stays ~8 mm (a third of an inch) inside each tag,
which clears the printer's dead edge AND hides a wobbly cut. 5 cuts → 10 tags
(1 down the middle, 4 across).

NOT CLAIMED: any Canva plan price; whether one data field can be linked to several
boxes (the page doesn't say); any printer's card-weight limit (we say "check your
printer's manual"); Canva Print tag products.

Target: ~1,860 words @ 3.70 w/s ≈ 8 min 40 s. Ad breaks after S05 and S09.

## S01 — HOOK (~120 words)

> Bulk Create will happily put thirty names on thirty gift tags — and print them
> on thirty sheets of card.
>
> Canva's help page says it plainly: each row of your names list makes one page.
> And when you print, one page is one sheet of paper. One little tag on every
> sheet.
>
> The fix is one change to the names list. Ten name columns instead of one, so each
> row fills a whole sheet. Thirty names becomes three rows, and three sheets.
>
> There is a free names sheet in the description, already set up with the ten
> columns, plus a one-page cutting guide. Let me build the sheet properly, and get
> all ten tags out of it with five cuts.

## S02 — THE FREE SHEET, AND THREE PRINCIPLES (~110 words)

> The free names sheet and cutting guide are linked below — the ten columns ready to
> fill, and the five cuts drawn out on one page.
>
> Three principles.
>
> One: design the sheet, not the tag. The paper is what goes through your printer, so
> the paper is the design.
>
> Two: fill the page. Ten tags, edge to edge, no gaps. The edge of the paper becomes
> the edge of your tags, and you never cut it.
>
> Three: keep everything well inside. A border of plain space around every tag hides
> the edge your printer cannot reach, and the cut that wanders.
>
> Sheet first, fill the page, keep it inside.

## S03 — THE SHEET (~145 words)

> Start with the paper you actually print on.
>
> In Canva, create a design at the size of your paper. In the US that is Letter —
> custom size, eight and a half by eleven inches. Everywhere else, search for A
> four.
>
> Now the grid. File, Rulers and guides, Add guides. Choose Custom. Set two columns,
> and five rows, and add the guides.
>
> That is your ten tags. Two across, five down, filling the whole page.
>
> On Letter, each tag comes out at four and a quarter by two point two inches. On A
> four, a hundred and five by just under sixty millimetres. Both are proper gift tag
> sizes — big enough for a name you can read across the room, small enough to sit on
> a small present.
>
> Canva locks the guides for you, so they will not move while you work. They are
> your cutting plan, so leave them exactly where they are.

## S04 — THE FIRST TAG (~150 words)

> Design one tag first — the top left one — and get it right before you copy
> anything.
>
> Put a rectangle in the cell, and stretch it to the guides on all four sides. That
> is the tag. Give it a colour, or leave it white.
>
> Now the safe space. Everything that matters — the art, the name, the words — stays
> about a third of an inch inside the tag. Eight millimetres. Two reasons.
>
> Your printer cannot print right to the edge of the paper. Most home printers lose
> three to six millimetres on every side. And when you cut, you will not cut exactly
> on the line. Nobody does. A plain border means a wobble of two millimetres simply
> does not show.
>
> Add the art at one end — a tree, a star, holly, whatever you like. Leave the other
> end clear for the hole. Then two lines of text: To, and From.

## S05 — THE TEN (~155 words)

> Now turn one tag into ten.
>
> Select everything on the first tag, and group it. Then duplicate the group, and drag
> the copy into the cell next door, lined up on the guides.
>
> Do it again down the page, until all ten cells are full. Ten identical tags, edge to
> edge.
>
> The guides are perfect for building, but guides do not print. So you need cut lines
> you can actually see on the paper.
>
> Draw a thin line along every guide — one down the middle of the page, four across.
> Make them pale grey and very thin. Bold black lines are a mistake, because every
> cut that drifts off the line leaves a black sliver on the edge of a tag.
>
> Pale grey, very thin, on the guides. And no lines round the outside of the page —
> that edge is the paper's edge, and it is already cut for you.
>
> Now the names, and the trap that turns three sheets into thirty.

## S06 — THE TRAP: ONE ROW, ONE PAGE (~205 words)

> Here is the way almost everyone does names.
>
> You make a single tag. You open Bulk Create. You add a list, one column, called
> Name, and thirty names down it. You connect the column to the name on the tag, and
> you click Create.
>
> And it works. Thirty tags, every name spelled right.
>
> Then you look at the page count. Thirty pages. Canva's help page explains why: each
> row of your data makes one page. Thirty rows, thirty pages. And your printer treats
> every page as a sheet of paper. Thirty sheets of card, each with one little tag on
> it, and a lot of cutting.
>
> Here is the fix. Your sheet already has ten tags on it. So give it ten name boxes,
> and ten name columns.
>
> In the list, call the columns Name one, Name two, all the way to Name ten. Each row
> is now one SHEET, not one tag. The first ten names go across the first row. The
> next ten across the second row. The last ten across the third.
>
> Connect Name one to the first tag, Name two to the second, and so on round the
> sheet.
>
> Click Create. Three pages. Three sheets of card. Thirty names. The help page allows
> up to a hundred and fifty fields, so ten is nowhere near the limit.

## S07 — THE NAMES LIST (~145 words)

> The names list deserves two minutes of care, because it is the part you will
> reuse.
>
> Make it in a Canva Sheet, or in any spreadsheet saved as a CSV. Ten columns, Name
> one to Name ten. Read the names across each row, left to right, the way they will
> land on the sheet — top left first, then across, then down.
>
> If thirty-four people are on the list, you get four rows, and the last row has four
> names and six empty cells. That is fine. Those six tags come out with a blank To
> line, ready to write on by hand for the person you forgot.
>
> The From line does not need a column. It says the same thing on every tag, so type
> it once on the first tag before you copy it.
>
> In Bulk Create, choose Match and fill fields, and check every box went to the right
> column before you create.

## S08 — THE FREE WAY (~135 words)

> Now the bit the tutorials skip. Bulk Create is not on Canva Free.
>
> Canva's help page lists Canva Pro, Teams, Business, Enterprise, Education and
> Nonprofit. If you are on the free plan, the button is not there for you.
>
> You can still make every one of these tags. The sheet, the grid, the ten tags and
> the cut lines are all free features.
>
> For the names, type them. Click into each name box, type the name, move to the next.
> Ten names a sheet takes a couple of minutes. When the sheet is full, duplicate the
> whole page, and type the next ten over the top.
>
> Thirty names is three pages and maybe ten minutes of typing. Bulk Create saves real
> time at two hundred names. At thirty, the free way is perfectly good.

## S09 — DOWNLOAD AND PRINT (~155 words)

> Now get it onto paper at the right size.
>
> Download as PDF Standard. That is the one for a home printer.
>
> You will see PDF Print, with an option for crop marks. Canva's help page says crop
> marks only appear in PDF Print downloads, and they are off unless you turn them on.
> But crop marks mark the edge of the page, and on this sheet the edge of the page is
> already a cut edge. They tell you nothing you need. Your pale grey lines do that
> job.
>
> In the print window, find the scale setting, and set it to one hundred percent, or
> Actual size. Not Fit to page. Fit to page shrinks the whole sheet, and every tag
> with it.
>
> Then print one copy on plain paper first. Hold it up against a sheet of card, and
> check the art sits well inside every tag. Only then load the card — and check the
> thickest card your printer can take in its manual.

## S10 — FIVE CUTS (~140 words)

> Ten tags, five cuts. Here is the order.
>
> First, the long cut, straight down the middle of the page, on the line. You now have
> two strips of five tags.
>
> Then take each strip, and cut across on the four lines. Five tags fall off each
> strip.
>
> That is it. The outside edges were cut by the paper factory, so you never touch
> them.
>
> A paper trimmer makes this quick and straight, but scissors are fine. The plain
> border round every tag is there so you do not have to be perfect.
>
> Then punch the hole in the clear end you left — not too close to the edge, or the
> ribbon tears it. Thread through ribbon or twine.
>
> And write the From line in your own hand if you left it blank. People notice a
> handwritten tag.

## S11 — ALL YEAR (~125 words)

> Here is why this is worth setting up properly once.
>
> The sheet is the system. The grid, the safe border, the ten name boxes and the cut
> lines do not care what the art is.
>
> So in January, duplicate the design, and swap the Christmas tree for balloons.
> That is your birthday sheet. Swap it for a simple leaf, and you have thank-you tags.
> A plain one with just To and From works for anything.
>
> And keep the names list. Next December, you open it, cross out two names, add three,
> and click Create.
>
> Every gift you give in the year can go out with a tag that looks like it came from
> the same house. It costs a sheet of card each time.
