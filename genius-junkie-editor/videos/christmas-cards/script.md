# How to Make Christmas Photo Cards in Canva (Print-Ready 5×7)

## Facts verified 2026-10-29

- **THE TRAP — the free shipping is the slow one.** Canva Help Centre, "Estimate
  delivery time for print orders" (read 29 Oct 2026): *"Production takes 1–2
  business days for most products."* *"If you place your order after 5:00 PM local
  time, production may take an extra business day."* US shipping: **Express 2–3,
  Standard 5–8, Economy 7–14 business days**. *"Delivery timeframes are in business
  days: Monday to Friday, excluding public holidays..."* *"The most accurate
  delivery timeframe for your order is always the one shown at checkout."*
  Canva's US **Folded Cards** page (29 Oct 2026) says something longer: Economy
  delivery *"FREE"*, *"11-20 days"*; Standard *"7-12 days"*; Express *"5-5 days"*.
  (The research agent saw "9-20 days" there on 28 Oct — it moves.) The two pages
  disagree, so the script uses the LONGER (product-page) numbers, read as business
  days, and says the checkout date is the one that counts.
- **USPS** "Postal Service Recommends 2026 Holiday Mailing and Shipping Dates"
  (22 Sep 2026): for delivery by 25 Dec in the contiguous US, *"First-Class Mail:
  Dec. 17"*, Ground Advantage Dec. 17, Priority Mail Dec. 18.
- **Royal Mail** "Get ready for Christmas 2026" (read in a browser, 29 Oct 2026):
  last UK posting dates **2nd Class Friday 18 December**, **1st Class Monday 21
  December**.
- **OUR WORKED ORDER-BY DATES (US, our arithmetic, labelled as such on screen):**
  cards in your hands by **Fri 11 Dec** gives a week to write and post them by
  USPS's 17 Dec. Counting back business days (2 production + the product page's
  top shipping number), skipping Thanksgiving Thu 26 Nov: **Economy (free) — order
  by Tue 10 Nov. Standard — by Fri 20 Nov. Express — by Wed 2 Dec.** Order before
  5 PM. UK: Canva's help gives Standard 5–8 and Express 2–3 business days; cards in
  hand by Tue 15 Dec for 2nd Class on the 18th → Standard by Mon 30 Nov, Express by
  Mon 7 Dec. The UK cards page gives no delivery times for its free delivery, so the
  UK free option is "check at checkout".
- **THE CARD.** Canva US Folded Cards: sizes *"Portrait - 5 x 7in"*, *"Landscape -
  7 x 5in"*, 4.25 × 5.5; papers Premium, Deluxe, Hemp; finishes Matte, Gloss,
  Uncoated, Gloss and matte. Neither the US nor the UK page says envelopes are
  included — envelopes are listed as a separate product.
- **BLEED.** Canva Help, "Margins, bleed and crop marks": File → Rulers and guides →
  Show margins; File → Settings → Show print bleed. *"0.125 inches (3.175 mm) on all
  sides for products that trim or get cut"*, *"cannot be adjusted"*. *"Keep text and
  logos here."* *"Extend backgrounds here, not text."*
- **LANDSCAPE BACK COVER.** Canva Help, "Print in the correct orientation": on
  landscape folded cards *"the panel for the back cover needs to be inverted"*. The
  page only says this for landscape.
- **UK LETTER SIZE** — Canva's UK cards page: standard cards fit the Letter rate,
  *"up to 24 x 16.5cm and 5mm thick"* (Canva's statement, not Royal Mail's page).

NOT CLAIMED: any card price (the US page's own numbers disagree with each other),
whether envelopes come with the cards, the UK free-delivery time, what Canva does
with a late order, any Canva plan price.

## S01 — HOOK (~120 words)

> Canva's free shipping on printed cards is the slow one — and it can turn your
> Christmas cards into New Year cards.
>
> Canva's own folded cards page lists economy delivery as free, and eleven to
> twenty days. Standard is seven to twelve. Express is five. And production comes
> on top, one to two business days.
>
> In the US, the post office's last day for First-Class Mail before Christmas is
> the seventeenth of December. Count back, and free shipping means ordering by
> about the tenth of November.
>
> There is a free order-by calendar in the description, with the dates worked out.
> Let me build the card properly, and get it ordered in time.

## S02 — THE FREE CALENDAR, AND THREE PRINCIPLES (~115 words)

> The free calendar is linked below — the order-by date for every shipping option,
> the post office dates for the US and the UK, and the checks, on one page.
>
> Three principles.
>
> One: pick the shipping date first, and design second. The design can be done in
> an evening. The calendar cannot be moved.
>
> Two: one photo, big. A Christmas card is seen for three seconds on a fridge door.
> One good photo beats a collage of six small ones.
>
> Three: trust the mock-up and the checkout. The mock-up shows what will print. The
> checkout shows when it will arrive.
>
> Date first, one photo, trust the checkout.

## S03 — PICK THE CARD (~140 words)

> Start with the card, not the design.
>
> In Canva, go to Print, and choose folded cards. Canva's folded cards come in a
> few sizes — five by seven inches, portrait or landscape, and a smaller four and a
> quarter by five and a half.
>
> Choose five by seven, portrait. It is the size most greeting cards are, and Canva's
> UK page calls seven by five the best size for a card.
>
> Portrait matters for one more reason. Canva's help page says that on a landscape
> folded card, the panel for the back cover has to be turned upside down to print
> the right way up. The help page only gives that warning for landscape cards, so
> portrait is the simpler choice.
>
> Now pick a template, or start blank. A template is fine — you are going to replace
> almost everything on the front anyway.

## S04 — THE PHOTO (~150 words)

> The photo is the card, so get the photo right first.
>
> Use the original photo, from your phone or your camera. Not a screenshot, and not
> the copy someone sent you in a chat — those have been shrunk, and a shrunk photo
> prints soft.
>
> Upload it. Then drag it onto the front panel, and let it fill the whole panel,
> edge to edge.
>
> Double-click the photo to crop it. Put the faces in the top two thirds, and leave
> some quiet space — sky, a wall, a blurred background — where the words will go.
>
> One photo, big, faces up. If you have six good photos and cannot choose, that is
> what the inside of the card is for. The front gets one.
>
> And check every face is actually looking at the camera. A photo that looks fine
> on a phone screen is suddenly very obvious at five by seven inches.

## S05 — THE FRONT (~160 words)

> Now the words on the front, and the two lines that keep them safe.
>
> First, turn on the guides. File, Rulers and guides, Show margins. Then File,
> Settings, Show print bleed. You now have two boxes on the page.
>
> Canva's help page is clear about what goes where. Keep text and logos inside the
> margin. Extend backgrounds to the bleed — not text. The bleed is an eighth of an
> inch on every side, three point one seven five millimetres, and Canva says it
> cannot be adjusted.
>
> So the photo stretches all the way out to the bleed. The words stay inside the
> margin.
>
> Add one line: Merry Christmas, or Happy Holidays. Under it, the family name, and
> the year — twenty twenty-six. Two fonts at most. One big, one small.
>
> Put the words on the quiet part of the photo. If they sit on something busy, add
> a soft shape behind them, rather than a heavy outline around the letters.

## S06 — THE TRAP, DEMONSTRATED (~210 words)

> Here is the order most people place, so you can see exactly where it goes wrong.
>
> The design is done on the twenty-fifth of November. Into the cart, free economy
> shipping, place the order. Production, one to two business days. Economy, up to
> twenty days on Canva's folded cards page.
>
> Now count business days. Thanksgiving is a holiday, and weekends don't count. The
> cards land in the middle of December, or later — and you still have to write
> them, address them and post them.
>
> The post office's own date for First-Class Mail to arrive by Christmas is the
> seventeenth of December. That is the line.
>
> Now the same order, placed on purpose. Give yourself a week to write the cards, so
> they need to be in your hands by Friday the eleventh of December. Count back.
>
> Free economy shipping: order by Tuesday the tenth of November. Standard: by Friday
> the twentieth of November. Express: by Wednesday the second of December. Those are
> our sums from Canva's own numbers — and order before five in the afternoon, because
> Canva says a later order can add a day.
>
> In the UK, Royal Mail's last date for second class is Friday the eighteenth of
> December, and first class is the twenty-first.
>
> And whatever the calendar says, Canva's help page says the most accurate date is
> the one shown at checkout. Look at it before you pay.

## S07 — THE INSIDE (~140 words)

> Open the card. The inside is two panels, and the right-hand one does the work.
>
> Put the message on the right-hand inside panel. Something short — wishing you
> a very merry Christmas and a happy New Year — and leave space underneath to sign
> by hand.
>
> That handwritten line matters more than anything printed. A printed signature
> looks like a business sent it.
>
> The left-hand inside panel is where the other photos go, if you have them. Three
> or four small ones, or a single line about the year — the new house, the new baby,
> the trip.
>
> Or leave it empty on purpose. For the people you are closest to, an empty panel
> is room for a proper note, and that is the card they keep.
>
> Keep the inside the same as the front: the same two fonts, the same colours. And
> keep everything inside the margins here too. The fold runs down the middle, and
> anything that crosses it gets bent in half.

## S08 — THE BACK (~130 words)

> The back is the panel almost nobody designs, and the one people turn over first.
>
> Keep it small. The family name and the year, small, near the bottom, centred.
> Maybe a tiny version of the photo.
>
> If you chose a landscape card after all, this is where it catches you. Canva's
> help page says that on a landscape folded card, the back cover panel needs to be
> inverted — turned upside down — so it reads the right way up when the card stands.
> There is a whole video on why folding does that, linked at the end.
>
> On a portrait card, check the mock-up anyway. It is the only place you see the
> card as it will actually be folded, and it costs nothing to look.

## S09 — ORDERING IT (~160 words)

> Now order it.
>
> Select Print, and Canva shows you the options for folded cards. The paper —
> premium, deluxe or hemp. The finish — matte, gloss, uncoated, or gloss and matte.
>
> For a photo card, gloss makes the photo pop, and matte is usually easier to write on. If
> you are signing every card by hand, that is worth thinking about.
>
> Then the quantity. Count your list before you choose, and add a few — someone
> always gets missed.
>
> Then the mock-up. Canva shows the inside and the outside. Go through every
> panel. Faces in frame, words inside the margins, nothing sitting across the fold.
>
> Then the shipping option. This is the step that matters. Pick the option against
> today's date and the calendar — and read the delivery date at checkout before you
> pay. If it lands after the eleventh of December, pay for the faster option, or
> accept they will be late.

## S10 — ENVELOPES AND POSTING (~140 words)

> Before you celebrate, the envelopes.
>
> Neither Canva's US nor UK cards page says envelopes come with the cards, and Canva
> lists envelopes as a separate product. So check the product page before you order,
> and if they are not included, order envelopes at the same time.
>
> If you are in the UK, Canva's cards page says a standard card fits the Letter
> rate — up to twenty-four by sixteen and a half centimetres, and five millimetres
> thick. A five by seven card in its envelope sits inside that.
>
> Canva's UK page also offers sending cards straight to the recipient's address. For
> someone who lives far away, that saves a trip — but it skips your handwritten
> line, so use it for the few you cannot sign in person.
>
> Then the stamps, and the post box, before the post office's date. Count the
> stamps against the list the same way you counted the cards, because running out
> of stamps on the last posting day is the other way these end up late.

## S11 — NEXT YEAR (~125 words)

> Once this card works, keep it.
>
> Next November, open the same design, duplicate it, and change three things —
> the photo, the year, and the line on the inside. The sizes, the margins and the
> layout are already right.
>
> And put a reminder in your calendar now, for the first week of November next year.
> That is the one step that would have saved most late cards.
>
> If you make cards for other people — family, a small business, a club — the same
> rule applies, with more of them. Every extra card you sign adds a day before the
> post box. Build that into the order date, not the night before.
>
> One design, duplicated each year, ordered in the first half of November.

## S12 — THE CHECKS (~110 words)

> Before you pay, seven checks.
>
> One. Five by seven, portrait.
>
> Two. The photo fills the front, out to the bleed.
>
> Three. All the words are inside the margins.
>
> Four. The inside message leaves space to sign.
>
> Five. You read the delivery date at checkout, and it is before the eleventh of
> December.
>
> Six. You went through the mock-up, outside and inside.
>
> Seven. You know whether envelopes are included.
>
> The fifth is the one nobody does, because the free option is already selected.
> Free and late is not free.
