## S03 — CANVAS SETUP (~110 words)

> Now the canvas, and this matters if you're outside the US: American
> teachers print on **letter size** — eight and a half by eleven inches —
> not A4. Sell an A4 file and your reviews will say "it doesn't print
> right." In Canva, click Create a design, Custom size, switch the units to
> inches, and type eight point five by eleven. Portrait. Then set your
> margins: File, Settings, show margins — and keep everything half an inch
> from every edge, because school printers clip anything closer. That
> half-inch border is the difference between "prints perfectly" and a
> one-star review.

[VISUAL: Canva mock — Custom size dialog types 8.5 × 11 in; margin guides

## S04 — THE WORKSHEET DESIGN SYSTEM (~150 words)

> Every worksheet that sells shares the same skeleton, so build it once as
> your template. At the top: a header block — Name and Date on one line,
> because teachers grade these. Below that, the title, big and bold. Then
> one line of instructions in plain language — "Solve each problem and show
> your work." The middle is the body zone — that's where the actual
> activity lives. And at the bottom, a small footer with your store name
> and a copyright line. That footer is quiet marketing: every photocopy
> carries your brand. Two font rules: pick something a seven-year-old can
> read — no fancy scripts on the body — and check the licence. If you sell
> the file, every font must allow commercial use. Canva's built-in fonts
> are safe; fonts you upload might not be.

[VISUAL: skeleton assembles zone by zone with labels; font do/don't chips;
"commercial use ✓" licence card.]

## S05 — BUILD IT: AN ADDITION PRACTICE PAGE (~180 words)

> Let's build the classic: an addition practice page for second grade.
> In the body zone, click Elements and search "table". Insert a four by
> five table — that's twenty problems, the standard count. Now type your
> first problem in the top-left cell: twenty-four plus thirteen, and press
> tab to jump to the next cell. Fill the first row, then here's the speed
> trick: select the row, duplicate it, and just edit the numbers — you're
> not rebuilding the layout twenty times. Keep the font at least eighteen
> points so it's readable when photocopied. Add a small illustration from
> Elements if you like — one, in a corner, not scattered clip-art
> everywhere — and watch the little crown icon: crowned graphics are Pro
> content, and if you're on free Canva they'll watermark your export.
> Twenty problems, clean margins, one friendly graphic. That's a sellable
> page — and it took six minutes.

## S06 — FIVE VERSIONS, FAST (~120 words)

> One worksheet is a freebie. A *pack* is a product. Duplicate the page and
> change only the numbers — five pages of addition practice takes ten more
> minutes. Then go one step further: make an easy set, a medium set, and a
> challenge set. Teachers call that differentiation — one classroom, three
> ability levels — and packs that include it sell for more and get better
> reviews, because you've done the teacher's hardest prep job for them.
> Same skeleton, same design, different numbers. Your afternoon of work is
> now a fifteen-page differentiated pack — and that's a three-to-four
> dollar product, not a one-dollar page.

## S07 — ANSWER KEYS (~90 words)

> Now the part most beginners skip — and it's the part teachers check for
> before they buy: answer keys. Duplicate every worksheet page, keep the
> layout identical, and type the answers straight into the grid in red.
> Add "ANSWER KEY" to the header so nobody prints it by accident. It's ten
> minutes of typing, and it's the difference between "cute worksheet" and
> "I used this in class today" in your reviews. Teachers aren't buying
> your design — they're buying their Sunday evening back.

## S08 — EXPORT + THE SETTING THAT PROTECTS YOU (~150 words)

> Export time — and here's the setting I promised you. Share, Download,
> file type PDF Print. But before you click Download, open the options and
> tick **Flatten PDF**. Here's why this matters: an un-flattened PDF keeps
> your text and design as separate, selectable objects — a buyer can open
> it in free software, lift your layout, edit your worksheets, and resell
> them as their own. Flattening bakes every page into a single image layer.
> They get a perfect printable file; they don't get your working file.
> One checkbox — that's your entire copy protection. Name the file
> properly too: grade, topic, page count — "Grade 2 Addition Practice — 15
> Worksheets Plus Answer Keys" — because buyers see the file name after
> purchase, and it's your last impression.

[VISUAL: download dialog, Flatten toggle spotlight + camera push; side-by-side
