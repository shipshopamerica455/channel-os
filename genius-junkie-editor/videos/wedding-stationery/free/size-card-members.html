The one that costs money

“A7” is two completely different sizes.

  • A7 envelope · United States — 5.25 × 7.25 in. Holds a 5 × 7 card. This is the standard wedding invitation envelope.
  • A7 paper · ISO 216 — 74 × 105 mm (2.9 × 4.1 in). Holds nothing in this suite. Smaller than the reply card.

Order the envelope by its measurement, never by its name. The envelope must be bigger than the card in both directions, by about ⅛ to ¼ inch. If the two numbers look the same, stop.

The suite

  • Invitation — 5 × 7 in · 127 × 178 mm · A7 envelope (US)
  • Details card — 5 × 7 in · 127 × 178 mm · stacks with the invitation
  • Reply card — 3.5 × 5 in · 89 × 127 mm · A1 envelope, or a 4 × 6 postcard
  • Order of service — A5, 148 × 210 mm · folded from A4
  • Table number — A5, 148 × 210 mm · standing in a frame
  • Seating plan — A2 or A1, by guest count

Why ISO A7 is so small — it is arithmetic

A4 210×297  →  A5 148×210  →  A6 105×148  →  A7 74×105

Each size is the one above halved across the long side. A7 is not a choice.

Printing and posting

  • Bleed 0.125 in every side (5 × 7 becomes 5.25 × 7.25). Keep all type 0.25 in inside the trim.
  • Print at Actual size / 100%. Every print dialog defaults to Fit, which shrinks the card.
  • Print one and put it in the envelope before ordering any quantity.
  • Square envelopes, wax seals, ribbon and anything rigid are non-machinable and cost more to post.
  • No postage price is given here on purpose — rates change. Weigh one sealed invitation at the counter, then buy stamps.
  • Write the month as a word. 10/09/2027 is two different days depending on who reads it.
