## Pinned comment

> The one thing to take away, even if you skip the rest:
>
> **Share and Assign are not the same button.** A normal share link — edit,
> comment or view — points every student at the *same* design. Paste that into
> Google Classroom and thirty students are editing one file. **Assign** (top
> corner of the editor, or Share → Assign) gives every student **their own
> copy**, and their copy comes back to you.
>
> Two numbers from Canva's own docs that catch people out:
> · the assignment **link** stays live for **30 days**
> · the assignment **code** expires in **4 hours** — so generating one the night
>   before means it is dead by first period. Use the link for anything planned.
>
> And if you are not eligible for Canva Education (tutors, homeschool educators,
> lecturers, public librarians, teachers not currently in post — Canva's list,
> not mine): **Share → Template link** does the "own copy" half on a free
> account. You lose the marking side, not the teaching side.
>
> Full kit (six activity shells + the consent letter + the prompts):
> https://geniusjunkie.net/classroom-kit/
