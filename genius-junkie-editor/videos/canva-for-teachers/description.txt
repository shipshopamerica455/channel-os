Almost every problem teachers hit with Canva is one confusion, and it happens in
about week two: you make a good worksheet, you copy the link, you paste it into
Google Classroom — and thirty students open the SAME file. Student four deletes
a text box and it vanishes off everybody's screen, including the two who had
already finished.

That is not a bug. A share link shares one design.

🎯 **Share is for colleagues. Assign is for students.**
Edit, comment and view all point at the same file — right for co-planning with
another teacher, wrong for a class. **Assign** (the button in the top corner of
the editor, or Share → Assign) gives every student **their own copy**, and their
copy comes back to you when they submit it.

⏱️ **Two numbers Canva documents that catch people out:**
· the assignment **link** stays active for **30 days** (and there is a
  Deactivate link button)
· the assignment **code** expires in **4 hours** — so a code you set up the
  night before is dead by first period. Generate codes in the lesson; use the
  link for anything you plan ahead.

📥 **What the student does:** opens it, works in their own copy, presses Send to
teacher. Warn them about one behaviour or you will get a hand up every time —
**after they submit, their design becomes read-only**. If they need to change
something you request changes and they Resubmit; if they just want to keep
experimenting, Make a copy leaves the submission untouched.

✅ **Marking:** everything lands on Classwork as a status list, not an inbox —
Not submitted · To review · Done · Resubmission requested. Set your classes up
and you can filter by class. Feedback can be sticker comments dropped straight
onto the design, which younger classes actually read.

🏫 **If your school lives in an LMS** you do not have to leave it. Canva
integrates with Canvas, Schoology, D2L, Moodle, Blackboard, Google Classroom and
Microsoft Teams. Google Classroom takes up to 20 classes at once. One gotcha:
to assign the same design on a *different* platform, Canva says make a new copy
of it first.

🚩 **Check you are eligible before you build on it.** Canva Education is free and
worldwide, but the list is narrower than people assume. Not eligible, in Canva's
own words: qualified teachers not currently in a teaching position, public
librarians, preschool and daycare, colleges and universities and their staff,
adult and further education, extracurricular organisations, parents, and
homeschool educators who are not licensed or employed by a K-12 institution.

🔑 **And if you are on that list, you can still get the important half.** Share →
Template link: "anyone with the link can use this design as a template" — so
every learner opens their own copy on a free account. You lose Classwork and
submissions, not the teaching.

⏳ **The thing that actually saves a term:** build six reusable shells once — a
do-now, an exit ticket, a vocabulary page, a comparison grid, a labelled diagram
and a reading response — with no subject content in any of them. Then Tuesday is
typing this week's words into a page that already exists, and pressing Assign.
