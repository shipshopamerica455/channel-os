Six shells, and the button that gives every student their own copy

Almost every problem teachers hit with Canva is one confusion. You make a good worksheet, you paste the link into Google Classroom, and thirty students open the same file — student four deletes a text box and it vanishes off everybody's screen. This is the kit from the tutorial: the pages already built, and the workflow written down.

What's inside

  • Six editable Canva activity shells — a do-now, an exit ticket, a vocabulary page, a comparison grid, a labelled diagram frame and a reading response. A4, and deliberately empty of subject content.
  • The answer boxes are real empty boxes — a thin border and nothing in it, so a student types straight in rather than clearing your placeholder text first (which half of them won't).

  • The assign-and-collect walkthrough — Assign versus Share, link versus code, the marking statuses, and the Canvas / Schoology / D2L / Blackboard / Google Classroom routes.
  • A parental consent letter for under-13s — a real letter with a return slip, ready to paste onto your school's letterhead.
  • A term of prompts — twenty subject-neutral do-now and exit-ticket questions, so the shells are never staring at you blank.

The mistake this saves you from

A share link — edit, comment or view — points every student at one design. Assign gives every student their own copy, and their copy comes back to you. Share is for colleagues; Assign is for students. Get that one thing right and most of the pain disappears.

Two numbers from Canva's own documentation that catch people out: the assignment link stays live for 30 days, and the assignment code expires in 4 hours — so a code set up the night before is dead by first period.

If you're not eligible for Canva Education

Canva's own not-eligible list includes tutors and extracurricular organisations, homeschool educators who aren't licensed or employed by a school, lecturers and university staff, public librarians, parents, and qualified teachers who aren't currently in post. If that's you, the shells still work — and Share → Template link gives every learner their own copy on a free account. You lose the marking side, not the teaching side. The read-me spells it out.
