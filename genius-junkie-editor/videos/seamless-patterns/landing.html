The canvas edge is a crop, not a wrap

You can build a pattern that looks completely finished on your screen, export it, tile it, and watch a hard grid of seams appear — straight lines through your design every tile width, where the motifs stop dead at the boundary. Canva will not show you that seam. There is no offset preview, no tile preview, no wrap mode. The design looks perfect right up until it is on fabric.

The mistake this saves you from

Every element that touches an edge needs a twin on the opposite edge at exactly one canvas width or height away. Not roughly — exactly. Canva gives you the tool for it: Position → Advanced, and typed X and Y values measured in pixels from the top-left corner. Eyeballing it is what produces the seam, and a two-pixel misalignment is invisible on screen and obvious on a metre of cloth.

And the background has to run to the edge on all four sides. Spoonflower say so in their own guidance: "Be sure any background colors in your file extend to the edge of the file, otherwise the design will look broken up into separate squares as opposed to a seamless repeating layout."

Why patterns are worth the fuss

One seamless tile is not one product. It is fabric by the metre, and wallpaper, and wrapping paper. It is a digital paper pack. It is a background for packaging, for product photography, for a shop banner. It goes on a mug, a tote, a notebook cover. A file that meets itself can be scaled to any physical object without anyone having to think about where it ends — which is also why a pattern with a seam fails everywhere at once.
