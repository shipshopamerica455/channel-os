## Description

```
The canvas edge in Canva is a crop, not a wrap. Anything you push past the edge
is cut off there, and nothing appears on the other side to continue it.

Which means you can build a pattern that looks completely finished on your
screen, export it, tile it, and watch a hard grid of seams appear — straight
lines through your design every tile width, where the motifs stop dead at the
boundary. And Canva will not show you that seam. There is no offset preview, no
tile preview, no wrap mode. The design looks perfect right up until it is on
fabric.

The fix is exact arithmetic, and Canva does give you the tool for it: Position
→ Advanced, and typed X and Y values.

THE RULE
Every element touching an edge needs a twin on the opposite edge, placed by
TYPED coordinates — never dragged:
· crosses the RIGHT edge  → twin at X − canvas width
· crosses the BOTTOM edge → twin at Y − canvas height
The other coordinate must stay IDENTICAL. And a motif in a CORNER needs FOUR
copies — the original, one at X−width, one at Y−height, and one with both.

SPOONFLOWER'S PUBLISHED SPEC, AS AN EXAMPLE
· "Your files should be a JPG or PNG, saved at your preferred print dimensions
  and 150 DPI, and under 40 MB."
· "Spoonflower converts all files to 150 DPI when they are uploaded to the
  site." So the physical repeat size is your decision; the resolution is not.
· "Spoonflower prints using sRGB color mode" — not CMYK.
· "Be sure any background colors in your file extend to the edge of the file,
  otherwise the design will look broken up into separate squares as opposed to
  a seamless repeating layout."
Inches × 150 = pixels. An 8 inch repeat is 1200 px square.
Other marketplaces differ — check your own spec page.
```
