The one rule

The canvas edge is a CROP, not a wrap. Anything pushed past the edge is cut there and nothing continues on the other side. So every element touching an edge needs a twin, placed by typed coordinates — Canva Help Centre: X and Y are the distance in pixels from the top-left corner, set under Position → Advanced.

crosses the RIGHT edge   →   twin at  X − canvas width
crosses the BOTTOM edge  →   twin at  Y − canvas height

Mirror it for the left and top edges (X + width, Y + height). The other coordinate must stay identical — change both and the seam moves rather than going.

A motif in a corner needs FOUR copies: the original, one at X−width, one at Y−height, and one with both. Miss the fourth and you get a seam at every corner point, which is the most noticeable kind there is.

The seam test — the only honest check

You cannot judge a seam inside Canva: no tile preview, no offset preview, no wrap mode. Export the tile as a PNG, make a second design at exactly twice the tile size, and drop the PNG in four times — typed, not dragged:

X = 0     Y = 0        X = tile  Y = 0
X = 0     Y = tile     X = tile  Y = tile

Then look at the two joins. Fix it in the original, export again, re-test — never patch the proof sheet.

  • A hairline of white at a join → the background does not overhang the canvas edge.
  • A motif sliced in half → no twin, or the twin's other coordinate was changed.
  • A light-to-dark band → a gradient background; its dark end meets the next tile's light end.

The spec — Spoonflower, as an example

Your files should be a JPG or PNG, saved at your preferred print dimensions and 150 DPI, and under 40 MB.

They convert everything to 150 DPI on upload, so the physical repeat size is your decision and the resolution is not. They print in sRGB, not CMYK — and Canva works in RGB anyway, so there is nothing to change.

Be sure any background colors in your file extend to the edge of the file, otherwise the design will look broken up into separate squares as opposed to a seamless repeating layout.
inches × 150 = pixels
  • 2 in repeat → 300 × 300 px
  • 8 in repeat → 1200 × 1200 px
  • 12 in repeat → 1800 × 1800 px

Other marketplaces differ — check your own spec page.

Seven checks before you list it

  • The background covers the whole canvas and overhangs every side
  • Every edge-crossing motif has an exact twin — typed, not dragged
  • Every corner motif has all four copies
  • You have tiled it two by two and looked at BOTH joins
  • The pixels match the physical repeat at your marketplace's DPI
  • It is under their file size ceiling
  • Every motif came from a Canva search filtered to Free

The fourth is the only one that actually proves the first three. Arithmetic is a claim; tiling it is the evidence.
