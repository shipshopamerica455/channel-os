Six layouts, and the checkbox that keeps it private

A scrapbook is a stack of photographs and a lot of decisions. Two of those decisions are made before you place a single picture, and both are permanent: the page size, and whether the file you hand out still contains the parts of your photos you thought you had removed. This is the whole build from the tutorial, with the layouts already made.

The mistake this saves you from

Cropping in Canva is hiding, not deleting. The photograph is whole underneath and the crop is a window over it. That is fine while it lives in Canva — it stops being fine when you export. Canva's own help centre: "If you download a design containing cropped images to a PDF file, the PDF format will include the original image. This means someone could use a third-party tool (for example, a PDF editor) to get the full, uncropped image from your PDF export."

In a scrapbook that is a privacy problem rather than a design one. You cropped a former partner out of the holiday photo. You cropped a stranger's child out of the beach shot. All of it is still in the file you emailed to eleven people, and nothing on screen tells you. The fix is one checkbox — Flatten PDF — and the checklist in this pack tells you when to use it and the two things it costs you.

And the door that only opens one way

You cannot bring an exported scrapbook back into Canva and edit it. Canva turns an imported PDF into "individual images in an A4 document" — not editable pages, and not your page size. So the Canva design is the master copy permanently, and the page size you pick on page one is the page size you have. That is why this kit starts you at 12 × 12.
