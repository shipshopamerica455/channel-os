A scrapbook is a stack of photos and a lot of decisions. Two of those decisions
are made before you place a single picture, and both of them are permanent.

📐 **Decide the page size first.** The scrapbook standard is 12 × 12 inches —
that is what physical albums and page protectors are built around. If it is only
ever going on a screen or through a home printer, use 8.5 × 11 or A4 instead,
because a twelve-inch square page will not come out of a desktop printer without
being shrunk. Start in inches and let Canva do the pixel maths.

🚪 **The Canva design is the master copy, forever — not the PDF.** Canva's help
centre is explicit about what happens if you try to bring an exported album back
in: "the pages become individual images in an A4 document. It doesn't become a
page of editable text." So an exported scrapbook is a photograph of your work,
not your work. Keep the design and name it properly.

🚩 **The one almost nobody knows:** cropping in Canva is hiding, not deleting.
The photo is whole underneath and the crop is a window over it. Canva documents
what that means on export — "the PDF format will include the original image…
someone could use a third-party tool (for example, a PDF editor) to get the
full, uncropped image from your PDF export."

In a family album that is not a design problem, it is a privacy one. You cropped
a partner out. You cropped a stranger's child out of the beach shot. All of it
is still in the file you just emailed to eleven people, and nothing on screen
tells you.

✅ **The fix is one checkbox:** Share → Download → tick **Flatten PDF**. Canva:
flattening "converts the file into a static image, preventing editing in
software like Adobe Acrobat". Two costs to know about — the file gets bigger,
and accessibility tagging is removed, so leave it unticked if the document has
to work with a screen reader.

🖨️ **Exporting:** PDF Print is 300 dpi with bleed, crop marks and the colour
profile choice — that is the print-shop file. PDF Standard is 96 dpi and much
smaller — that is the one you send family. Bleed ON for a shop, OFF for a home
printer. On Canva Pro, Advanced settings → "download pages as separate files"
gives you a zip with one PDF per page, which is what most printers actually want.

📚 **Limits worth knowing:** images upload under 50 MB and no more than 250
million total pixels; a Canva Free folder holds 200 items and Pro holds 1000 (a
long family archive hits that cap well before it hits the 5 GB storage limit);
and a design maxes out at 350 pages — past that Canva's own advice is to build a
second design and merge the downloads.

⏱️ **And the part that saves the most time:** build six layouts, not twenty
pages. Delete the photos out of a finished spread, keep the frames, the text box
and the spacing, and duplicate it. The next album is an evening, not a weekend,
and every page matches because the geometry never moved.
