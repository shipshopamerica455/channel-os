The bleed — why every month prints with a white line

From the Canva Help Centre, "Use margins, bleed, rulers, and crop marks": "Canva adds 0.125 inches (3.175 mm) on all sides for products that trim or get cut" — and "Changing margin, bleed, and crop mark sizes are also not available. These are fixed and are measured based on our print specifications."

Turn it on: File → Settings → Show print bleed. A dashed border appears just outside your page.

A photo calendar is the one design where every page is a full-bleed photograph. So a photo dropped in to fit the page is three millimetres short on all four sides. On screen it looks perfect. The trimmer cuts through where your photo already ended — and no two copies are cut in exactly the same place, so the white sliver moves from edge to edge across the twelve months.

Canva's own fix: "resize or stretch your background to cover them." Drag the photo out past the dashed line on all four sides. It costs three millimetres of picture and it saves the whole calendar.

The pixels — check the long edge before you crop

page                300 dpi page     + Canva's bleed
A4   210 × 297 mm   2480 × 3508      2565 × 3583
A3   297 × 420 mm   3508 × 4961      3583 × 5036
Letter 8.5 × 11 in  2550 × 3300      2625 × 3375

Rule of thumb: about 2,500 px on the long edge for an A4 page — before you crop. Check the pixel dimensions in your file browser, not the megapixels and not how it looks on a phone.

A photo straight off a modern phone clears it easily. The ones that fail are the copies — anything that came through a messaging app, off social media, or by email. Those are compressed on the way, often to around 1,600 px, and it is invisible on screen. Go back to the original: the version in your camera roll, not the one somebody sent you back.

Too small? Use it inset with a border, or do not use it. Stretching it to fill is the third option and it is the one that looks cheap in print.

Canva's ceiling at the other end: uploads must be under 50 MB and no more than 250 million total pixels (JPEG, PNG, HEIC/HEIF, WebP) — which only bites on scans and panoramas.

The export — one correct answer

  • PDF Print — Canva: 300 dpi, "with bleed, crop marks, and color profile (RGB or CMYK) selection option". Tick crop marks and bleed.
  • Not PDF Standard — 96 dpi, prints soft.
  • Not JPG or PNG — thirteen separate files, no crop marks on any of them.

Then open the PDF and look at the corners: crop marks sitting outside the page, and photograph running all the way out to them. If the photo stops at the page edge, you did not stretch it.

Three questions for your printer

Ask before you design the thirteenth page, not after.

  • Does your bleed requirement match 3 mm? Most do — but ask, and if theirs differs, set the page size up differently rather than arguing with Canva's fixed bleed.
  • Do you want the PDF with crop marks, or without?
  • How do you bind it? Wire, staple and spiral all eat a different amount of the inside edge.

Seven checks before you export

  • Show print bleed is on, and every photo is stretched past the dashed line on all four sides
  • Every date sits inside the margin guide
  • The first weekday of all twelve months checked against a real calendar
  • Every photo was 2,500+ px on its long edge before you cropped it
  • Thirteen pages, including a cover
  • The week starts on the same day throughout
  • Exported PDF Print with crop marks — not PDF Standard

The first is the one that ruins all twelve months at once — and you cannot see it until they are in the box.
