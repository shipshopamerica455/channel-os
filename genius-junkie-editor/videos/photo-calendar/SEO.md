## The trap

**Canva adds the bleed outside your page, and it is not adjustable.**
Canva Help Centre, "Use margins, bleed, rulers, and crop marks": *"Canva adds
0.125 inches (3.175 mm) on all sides for products that trim or get cut"*, and
*"Changing margin, bleed, and crop mark sizes are also not available. These are
fixed and are measured based on our print specifications."*

A photo calendar is the one design where every page is a full-bleed photograph,
so a photo sized to *fit the page* is three millimetres short on all four sides
— and because no two copies are trimmed identically, the white sliver moves
from edge to edge across the twelve months. Canva's own fix, quoted on screen:
*"resize or stretch your background to cover them."*

**The second half** is the pixel cost of that drag: an A4 page at 300 DPI is
2,480 × 3,508, and with the bleed about 2,565 × 3,583 — so the rule is ~2,500 px
on the long edge **before** cropping. The photos that fail are the copies:
messaged, downloaded or emailed images are compressed to around 1,600 px and it
is invisible on screen.
