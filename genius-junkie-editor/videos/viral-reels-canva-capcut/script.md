# Create Viral Instagram Reels Using Canva + CapCut

**Target runtime:** 8:30–10:00 after the cut (≈1,900 narration words + demo beats)
**Format:** screen recording (Canva web → CapCut desktop), narrated as you click.
**Read this like you talk.** Every line is written to be spoken. The `[RECORD]`
blocks are what should be happening on screen while you say the lines above them.

**Recording setup before you start:**
- Record at 1920×1080 or higher, full screen, cursor visible.
- Canva open in one tab with a blank session (logged in, recent designs visible).
- CapCut desktop open behind it with a clean workspace.
- Have one finished example reel already exported so you can show it in the cold open.
- Don't worry about pauses, misclicks or restarts — the pipeline cuts them.
  If you fumble a sentence, just pause a beat and say the whole line again.

## PART 1 — CANVA: THE DESIGN (target ~3 min)

### 1.1 Set up the Reel canvas

> First thing: the canvas. In Canva, click Create a design, top right, and
> search for Instagram Reel. You want the video one — ten-eighty by
> nineteen-twenty, portrait. Don't grab a Story template or a Post and try to
> stretch it later, because your text will sit in the wrong safe zones and
> Instagram will crop right over it.

[RECORD] Click **Create a design** → type "Instagram Reel" → hover both results
so the sizes show → click the Reel video option. New editor opens.

> Before you touch anything else, look at the edges of the canvas. The top
> fifteen percent and the bottom twenty percent are danger zones — that's
> where Instagram lays its own interface, your caption, the audio tag, the
> share buttons. Anything important lives in the middle of the frame. Keep
> that in your head the whole way through.

[RECORD] Slowly move the cursor around the top and bottom edges of the blank
canvas as you describe the zones — the auto-zoom will follow the cursor.

### 1.2 Build the hook slide

> Slide one is your hook, and it has one job: stop the thumb. You've got well
> under a second, so we're doing big text, high contrast, no clutter. Click
> Text, add a heading, and type your hook. I'm using "3 Canva tricks nobody
> shows you" — but the formula is: a number, a promise, and a hint of a
> secret. Steal that.

[RECORD] Text panel → **Add a heading** → type the hook line. Drag it to
centre-frame.

> If you're staring at a blank heading wondering what to write, here are the
> three hook formulas that carry most viral reels. One: the number promise —
> "three Canva tricks nobody shows you". Two: the call-out — "if you sell on
> Etsy, stop making this mistake". You name your exact viewer and they
> physically cannot scroll past. Three: the forbidden one — "I wasn't going
> to share this". Curiosity does the rest. Write yours with one of those
> three and you're ahead of ninety percent of the feed before you've touched
> a single animation.

> Now make it impossible to ignore. Font matters more than you think — go
> bold, go heavy. I'm using Archivo Black. Bump the size until it nearly
> touches the edges, then set your two colours: one for the background, one
> for the text, and make them fight each other. Dark background, electric
> text. If your brand colours are soft pastels, this is the one place you
> betray them.

[RECORD] Change font to a heavy display font, crank the size, set background
colour (Design → background or a full-bleed rectangle), set text colour.
Show one bad low-contrast combo for a second, then the good one — the
before/after reads well on camera.

> One more layer: select the text, hit Effects, and add a Background — that
> little colour block behind the words. It keeps your hook readable even when
> the video behind it gets busy. That one click is doing more work than any
> transition you'll add later.

[RECORD] Select text → **Effects** → **Background** style → adjust roundness
and colour.

### 1.3 Build slides two to four — the value

> Now duplicate that slide three times — the little duplicate icon, right
> here. Same background, same fonts, same positions. That consistency is
> what makes it feel designed instead of thrown together, and it means the
> text lands in the same spot every slide, which matters when we animate.

[RECORD] Click **Duplicate page** three times so there are 4 pages in the
timeline strip.

> Slides two, three and four are your actual content — one idea per slide,
> one line of setup and one line of payoff. Nobody reads a paragraph in a
> reel. If a slide needs more than two short lines, it's two slides.

[RECORD] Type real content into slides 2–4 (the three tricks, one per slide).
Doesn't need to be fast — the cut will tighten it.

> Let's make it feel alive. Photos or graphics: click Elements, and if you're
> dropping a product shot or a face in, use Edit photo and Background remover
> — yes, that's now on the free tier — so it sits on your colour instead of
> inside a white box.

[RECORD] Elements → search for a relevant graphic/photo → place it →
**Edit photo** → **BG Remover** → position it.

### 1.4 Animate and export

> Animation time, and here's the rule: one animation style for the whole reel.
> Select the page, click Animate, and look at these — for reels you want the
> aggressive ones. Pop, Tumble, Breathe. I'm using Pop. Click it once and
> it applies to the page. Do the same on every slide. Skip the gentle fades —
> gentle reads as slow, and slow gets scrolled.

[RECORD] Select page → **Animate** → hover a few options so previews play →
click **Pop** → repeat quickly for the other slides.

> Now timing. Each slide's got a duration on it — click the little clock.
> Hook slide: one and a half seconds, no longer. Content slides: two to
> two-and-a-half seconds each. That feels brutally fast while you're editing
> and exactly right when you're scrolling.

[RECORD] Click the duration/clock control on each page and set: 1.5s / 2.5s /
2.5s / 2.5s. Show the total runtime readout.

> One last page before we export: duplicate your hook slide one more time and
> tweak it into a cover. This page never plays in the reel — it's the frame
> people see on your profile grid. Same hook text, but shrink it a touch and
> pull it dead centre, because the grid crops reels to a square. Thirty
> seconds now saves your profile from looking like a row of decapitated
> sentences.

[RECORD] Duplicate the hook page → nudge text to centre → point at where the
square grid-crop would fall.

> Export. Share, top right — Download — and here's the bit people miss: file
> type MP4 Video, not PNG, or you lose every animation you just set. Quality
> ten-eighty. Download it.

[RECORD] **Share** → **Download** → open the file-type dropdown deliberately →
select **MP4 Video** → 1080p → **Download**. Show the file landing in
Downloads.

> Canva's done its job: it's given us a clean, branded animation. But a Canva
> export on its own still feels like a slideshow. CapCut is where it starts
> feeling like a reel.

## PART 2 — CAPCUT: THE EDIT (target ~3.5 min)

### 2.1 Import and lay out the timeline

> Open CapCut, new project, and drag your Canva MP4 straight into the
> timeline. First job — check the frame. Ratio button says nine-sixteen,
> and because we designed at Reel size it should drop in perfectly, no black
> bars, no cropping.

[RECORD] CapCut desktop → **Create project** → drag the MP4 from Downloads
into the timeline → point at the ratio/resolution controls.

### 2.2 The music decision — this is the pro tip

> Now music, and this is the part that separates people who edit reels from
> people who post slideshows. We're going to edit to a beat. Grab the sound
> you want — CapCut's library is fine for editing — but remember what I said
> about export at the end, because we are not keeping this audio. It's
> scaffolding.

[RECORD] Audio panel → drop a beat-driven track under the clip.

> Right-click the track and hit beat detection — CapCut marks the beats as
> little dots on the audio. Now split your Canva clip so every slide change
> lands on a beat. Select the clip, playhead on the beat, split. When the
> visual hits the beat, the reel feels expensive. It's a five-minute job and
> it's the single biggest upgrade you can make.

[RECORD] Right-click audio → beat detection ON → zoom the timeline so the
beat dots are visible → split the video at slide boundaries and nudge each
cut onto a dot. Play it back once so the sync is audible.

### 2.3 Auto captions with a brand style

> If there's any voiceover or talking in your reel, captions are not optional
> — most people watch with the sound off. Text, Auto captions, generate.
> CapCut transcribes the whole thing in about ten seconds.

[RECORD] **Text** → **Auto captions** → generate → captions appear on the
timeline.

> Then style them once and it applies everywhere: pick a caption preset, but
> change the colours to yours — I'm matching the yellow from the Canva design
> so the whole reel feels like one thing. Bump the size, drag them to the
> lower third — inside that safe zone we talked about — and turn on the
> word-by-word animation so they pop as they're spoken.

[RECORD] Select a caption → template/style panel → set font, brand colours,
size, position; enable the karaoke/word highlight animation. Show one caption
with the wrong colour, then fix it.

> Before you move on, actually read them. Auto captions are about ninety-five
> percent right, and the five percent it misses is always the word that
> matters — your brand name, the product, the price. Click any caption on
> the timeline and fix the text right there. A typo in a caption reads as
> careless, and careless doesn't get shared.

[RECORD] Click through two or three captions, fix one deliberately wrong word.

### 2.4 Movement: keyframe punch-ins and one transition

> Even with Pop animations, static slides need camera energy. Here's the
> thirty-second version of a punch-in: playhead at the start of a slide,
> select the clip, open the scale keyframe, click the diamond. Jump to the
> end of the slide, scale it up to about a hundred and eight percent.
> That's it — a slow push-in on every slide. Subtle, constant motion.

[RECORD] Do the keyframe zoom on one slide: diamond at start (100%) →
playhead to end → scale 108%. Play it back. Then repeat on one more slide
at speed.

> Transitions: one. Pick one — I use a fast glitch or a whip — and put it
> only on the beat drop, the moment your hook slide smashes into the content.
> A reel with five different transitions looks like a wedding video from
> 2011. One transition, used once, looks intentional.

[RECORD] Transitions panel → drag the chosen one between slide 1 and 2 →
set it short (~0.3s) → play back the hook-to-content moment.

### 2.5 The loop ending

> Last trick before export, and this is the one almost nobody uses: make it
> loop. Take your last slide, and make its final frame visually identical to
> your first frame — same background, same layout. When the reel restarts,
> there's no seam. People watch it twice without noticing, your average watch
> time doubles, and watch time is the metric Instagram actually ranks on.

[RECORD] Copy the hook slide's look onto the tail of the last slide (or trim
the last clip and duplicate slide 1's segment at the end). Play the loop
point a couple of times.

### 2.6 Export — and the reach-killing mistake

> Export settings: ten-eighty P, sixty frames a second, bitrate on high —
> and now the thing I promised you at the start. Before you hit export,
> mute the music track. Yes, delete it or mute it. Here's why: if you bake
> a song into your file, Instagram hears it as just... your audio. But
> trending audio only helps your reach if it's added inside Instagram —
> that's how the algorithm files your reel under that sound. So: export
> silent — or voiceover only — then add the trending version of the track
> in the Instagram app when you post. You get the beat-synced edit AND the
> algorithm credit. That's the whole trick.

[RECORD] Mute/delete the music track deliberately — make this visually
obvious. **Export** → show 1080p / 60fps / high bitrate settings → export →
show the finished file.

> And when you do post it: the reel we just built, plus the trending audio
> at low volume under your voiceover, a cover frame that's readable as a
> thumbnail, and your hook line repeated as the first line of the caption.

[RECORD] Optional: quick shot of the export playing in QuickTime, or the
Instagram "add audio" screen on your phone if you want a bonus shot.

## THE 10-SECOND CHECKLIST (target ~40s)

> Quick checklist before this thing goes anywhere near your profile — run
> every reel through these five. One: does something move in the first
> second? Two: does it make sense on mute? Three: is every word inside the
> safe zone? Four: does the ending loop back to the start? Five: is the
> music added in Instagram, not baked into the file? Five yeses and you post
> it. Any no, and you now know exactly which two minutes of this video to
> rewatch.

[RECORD] The finished reel looping while you talk — or a simple five-line
checklist typed into a Canva page if you want it on screen.
